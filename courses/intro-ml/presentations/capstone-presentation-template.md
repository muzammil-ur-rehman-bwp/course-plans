# Capstone Presentation Template (Student-Facing)

**Target length:** 5–7 minutes + Q&A | **Given to students in Week 10 alongside the proposal
guidelines, used for the Week 16 presentation.**

## Required Slides
1. **Title** — project name, your name(s).
2. **Problem statement** — what are you predicting/classifying/clustering/recommending, and why
   does it matter?
3. **Data** — source, size, key characteristics (1–2 sentences + a sample or summary stat).
4. **Approach** — the at-least-three algorithm families you compared and why you chose them.
5. **Evaluation methodology** — your metric(s) and validation procedure (e.g., cross-validation),
   and why they fit the problem.
6. **Results** — a comparison table or chart across your models; which performed best and by how
   much.
7. **Tradeoff discussion** — at least one honest tradeoff between your compared models (e.g.,
   accuracy vs. interpretability, training time, sensitivity to preprocessing choices).
8. **Limitations & next steps** — one honest limitation and what you'd try with more time/data.
9. **Questions** — invite audience questions.

## Presentation Tips
- Practice the timing — 5–7 minutes goes faster than it sounds; cut detail, not clarity.
- Lead with the problem, not the code — the audience needs to care about the "why" before the
  "how."
- Show your comparison table/chart early — a project comparing multiple models is this course's
  defining capstone requirement, so make the comparison visible, not just mentioned.
- Be honest about what didn't work — the rubric (`assignments/capstone-rubric.md`) explicitly
  rewards sound analysis over simply claiming success.
