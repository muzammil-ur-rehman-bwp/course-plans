# Capstone Presentation Template (Student-Facing)

**Target length:** 5–7 minutes + Q&A | **Given to students in Week 11 alongside the proposal
guidelines, used for the Week 16 presentation.**

## Required Slides
1. **Title** — project name, your name(s).
2. **Problem statement** — what are you classifying/predicting, and why does it matter?
3. **Data** — source, size, key characteristics (1–2 sentences + a sample or summary stat).
4. **Architecture** — the network (MLP or CNN) and framework you used, and why you chose it.
5. **Training setup** — optimizer, regularization, and key hyperparameters.
6. **Results** — training/validation curves and test performance.
7. **Ablation/comparison** — the one isolated design choice you compared (e.g., with vs. without
   dropout, or optimizer A vs. B), with both conditions' results shown side by side.
8. **Limitations & next steps** — one honest limitation and what you'd try with more time/data.
9. **Questions** — invite audience questions.

## Presentation Tips
- Practice the timing — 5–7 minutes goes faster than it sounds; cut detail, not clarity.
- Lead with the problem, not the code — the audience needs to care about the "why" before the
  "how."
- The ablation/comparison slide (7) is required, not optional — it is what the rubric
  (`assignments/capstone-rubric.md`) specifically rewards beyond a single trained model.
- Be honest about what didn't work — a well-analyzed negative or mixed ablation result scores as
  well as a clearly positive one.
