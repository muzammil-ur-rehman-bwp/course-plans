# Presentation: Module 4 — Foundational Debates, Research Methods & the Capstone (Weeks 11–16)

**Format:** Slide deck outline for lecture delivery (convert to slides in institution's template).

1. **Title slide** — Advanced Artificial Intelligence (Post Graduate): Module 4, Foundational
   Debates, Research Methods & the Capstone
2. **Module 3 recap** — bridge from interpretability's post-hoc/mechanistic gap to the deeper
   question of what "understanding" means for a computational system at all
3. **The symbol grounding problem** — Harnad's circularity argument; relevance to learned vs.
   hand-specified representations
4. **The frame problem** — McCarthy & Hayes's axiom-explosion argument; relevance to MDP
   transition models and learned world models (Week 4 callback)
5. **The Chinese Room argument** — Searle's thought experiment, reconstructed premise by premise
6. **The three standard rebuttals** — the Systems Reply, the Robot Reply (linking back to
   symbol grounding), the Brain Simulator Reply, argued even-handedly
7. **What would settle the question?** — empirical question vs. conceptual/definitional dispute,
   structured-debate framing
8. **Scoping a research proposal** — the precise, falsifiable problem statement; the
   vague-topic failure mode
9. **Related-work survey standards** — accurate representation; relating papers to each other;
   motivating a specific, stated gap
10. **The feasibility argument** — what must be true, the main risk named honestly, why the risk
    is judged manageable
11. **Current frontier topics survey** — the fast-moving-area framing; problem-statement
    extraction practiced on a current paper
12. **The research proposal capstone** — the four required components, diagram
13. **Peer-review workshop & capstone rubric** — the 40%-weighting breakdown; the
    thesis-committee-style feedback protocol
14. **Module 4 & course recap** — the full 16-week course map, one slide
15. **Where to go next** — pointer to the sibling postgraduate courses (ANN/ML/DL/KR&R, Advanced)
    and to an actual thesis or dissertation trajectory

**Speaker notes:** slides 8–10 (research methods) are this module's technical core and should be
delivered with the same rigor as Module 1's regret-bound derivation — a problem statement or
feasibility argument is judged, not merely described; walk through the Week 15 worked
weak-vs-strong feedback example live rather than only summarizing it.
