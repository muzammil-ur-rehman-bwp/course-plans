# Presentation: Module 3 — AI Safety, Alignment & Interpretability (Weeks 8–10)

**Format:** Slide deck outline for lecture delivery (convert to slides in institution's template).

1. **Title slide** — Advanced Artificial Intelligence (Post Graduate): Module 3, AI Safety,
   Alignment & Interpretability
2. **Module 2 recap** — bridge from aligning self-interested agents' incentives (mechanism
   design) to aligning one learned system's objective with its designer's intent
3. **Specification gaming & reward hacking** — the Goodhart's-law framing; the CoastRunners case
   study
4. **The alignment problem, formalized** — the designer's true goal g vs. the specified proxy R;
   why greater capability makes gaming more effective, not less
5. **Outer vs. inner alignment** — the mesa-objective concept; why inner-alignment failures are
   harder to detect than outer-alignment failures
6. **Midterm logistics slide** — scope (Weeks 1–8), format, review-session pointer
7. **Reward modeling** — Bradley-Terry pairwise-preference learning; the training-data-dependent
   limitation, stated plainly
8. **Scalable oversight** — debate and iterated amplification, at the level of general mechanism
9. **Interpretability as a safety tool** — why external behavior alone may not reveal a
   misaligned mesa-objective
10. **Feature attribution** — gradient-based saliency, permutation importance, the LIME/SHAP
    surrogate-model family
11. **Mechanistic interpretability** — reverse-engineering circuits/features vs. characterizing
    input-output behavior
12. **The post-hoc vs. genuine-understanding gap** — the randomization sanity check, and what a
    method failing it actually shows
13. **Module 3 recap** — key results checklist (outer/inner alignment distinction, the
    reward-modeling limitation, the sanity-check evidence)
14. **Looking ahead** — "Next: the foundational debates and the research-proposal methodology"
    teaser slide

**Speaker notes:** this module is explicitly framed as technical research, not an ethics
afterthought — resist letting discussion drift into unstructured opinion; every claim here
(CoastRunners, the sanity-check findings) traces to a specific documented case or study, and the
module's rigor depends on keeping that grounding visible throughout.
