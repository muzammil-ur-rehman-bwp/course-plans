# Lab Manual 12 — Position Paper: The Chinese Room & Understanding

**Duration:** 3 hours (critical-writing/discussion format) | **Prerequisite:** Week 12 lecture

## Objectives
Reconstruct the Chinese Room argument precisely, take and defend a reasoned position on whether
behavioral indistinguishability is sufficient evidence for understanding, and engage a genuine
opposing counterpoint.

## Setup
No code environment is required this week. Bring the Searle (1980) handout excerpts and the
standard-replies summary from lecture. Create a single document `lab12_position_paper.md` (or
`.pdf`).

## Procedure
1. **Task A — Argument reconstruction:** reconstruct the Chinese Room argument's four premises
   and conclusion precisely, in your own words. State exactly which premise each of the three
   standard rebuttals (Systems Reply, Robot Reply, Brain Simulator Reply) targets.
2. **Task B — Position statement:** state, in one paragraph, your position on whether behavioral
   indistinguishability is sufficient evidence for genuine understanding.
3. **Task C — Position paper draft (500–700 words):** defend your Task B position. Explicitly
   state which of the three standard rebuttals (if any) your position depends on, and what
   evidence (if any) could in principle change your mind — or state explicitly why no evidence
   could, if that is your view.
4. **Task D — Peer exchange and rebuttal:** exchange with a peer who holds (or is assigned) the
   opposing position. Write a 3–5 sentence rebuttal-response addressing their single strongest
   counterpoint, appended to your Task C draft.

## Expected Output
A single document containing Tasks A–D as clearly labeled sections, ending with the Task D
rebuttal-response appended to the Task C draft.

## Submission
Submit `lab12_position_paper.md` (or `.pdf`) via the course submission system by the end of the
lab session.
