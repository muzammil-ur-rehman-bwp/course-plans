# Lab Manual 11 — Position Paper: Symbol Grounding & the Frame Problem

**Duration:** 3 hours (critical-writing/discussion format) | **Prerequisite:** Week 11 lecture

## Objectives
Connect one modern AI system design choice to the symbol grounding problem, the frame problem,
or both, and produce a reasoned, evaluated position — not a historical summary — on how
satisfying that design choice's implicit answer actually is.

## Setup
No code environment is required this week. Bring the Harnad (1990) and McCarthy & Hayes (1969)
handout excerpts from lecture. Create a single document `lab11_position_paper.md` (or `.pdf`).

## Procedure
1. **Task A — Problem restatement:** in your own words, restate the symbol grounding problem and
   the frame problem precisely enough that a reader unfamiliar with this week's lecture could
   follow your argument from your restatement alone.
2. **Task B — Design-choice analysis:** choose one modern AI system design choice (e.g., a
   learned world model, an RL agent's raw sensor-vector state representation, a large model's
   token-level internal representations) and identify, concretely, which design choice functions
   as an implicit answer to symbol grounding and/or the frame problem.
3. **Task C — Position paper draft (500–700 words):** write the position paper arguing whether
   your identified design choice is a satisfying answer or merely a practical workaround that
   sidesteps the deeper question, citing Harnad (1990) and/or McCarthy & Hayes (1969) precisely
   (not just by name).
4. **Task D — Peer exchange and rebuttal:** exchange drafts with a peer. Each of you identifies
   one point in the other's position where the opposite argument could reasonably be made.
   Append a 3–5 sentence rebuttal-response to your own paper addressing the counterpoint you
   received.

## Expected Output
A single document containing Tasks A–D as clearly labeled sections, ending with the Task D
rebuttal-response appended to the Task C draft.

## Submission
Submit `lab11_position_paper.md` (or `.pdf`) via the course submission system by the end of the
lab session.
