# Week 12 Lecture Plan — Advanced Artificial Intelligence (Post Graduate)
## Topic: Foundational and Philosophical Debates II

**Duration:** 2 hours lecture + 3 hour research seminar/lab (critical-writing/discussion format)

### Learning Objectives (Bloom's Level)
1. Reconstruct the Chinese Room argument's premises and conclusion precisely. (*Understand,
   Analyze*)
2. Evaluate the standard rebuttals (Systems, Robot, Brain Simulator) even-handedly. (*Evaluate*)
3. Take and defend a reasoned position on what, if anything, could settle what "understanding"
   means for a computational system. (*Evaluate*)

### Lecture Structure
| Time | Segment | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | The Chinese Room | Searle's thought experiment, reconstructed premise by premise |
| 0:20–1:00 | The standard rebuttals | Systems Reply, Robot Reply (linking back to Week 11), Brain Simulator Reply — argued even-handedly |
| 1:00–1:35 | What would settle it? | Is this an empirical question or a conceptual/definitional one? Structured debate |
| 1:35–1:55 | Position drafting | Students draft and justify a one-paragraph position |
| 1:55–2:00 | Synthesis | Transition to Week 13's research-methods focus |

### Materials/Equipment
- Slides: "Foundational Debates II: The Chinese Room & Understanding"
- Handout excerpts: Searle (1980) and standard replies

### Formative Check (in-class)
State precisely which premise of the Chinese Room argument the Systems Reply targets, and which
premise (if any) the Robot Reply targets instead.

### Link to Lab/Assessment
Lab 12: structured critical-writing/discussion exercise on the Chinese Room argument (see
`lab-manuals/lab-12.md`).
