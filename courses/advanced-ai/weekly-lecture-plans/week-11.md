# Week 11 Lecture Plan — Advanced Artificial Intelligence (Post Graduate)
## Topic: Foundational and Philosophical Debates I

**Duration:** 2 hours lecture + 3 hour research seminar/lab (critical-writing/discussion format)

### Learning Objectives (Bloom's Level)
1. State the symbol grounding problem precisely, in Harnad's original terms. (*Understand*)
2. State the frame problem precisely, in McCarthy & Hayes's original terms. (*Understand*)
3. Evaluate whether and how each problem resurfaces in a modern AI system's design choices.
   (*Evaluate*)

### Lecture Structure
| Time | Segment | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | Framing | Why these are treated as live research questions here, not historical trivia |
| 0:15–0:50 | Symbol grounding | Harnad's argument; relevance to learned vs. hand-specified representations |
| 0:50–1:25 | The frame problem | McCarthy & Hayes's argument; relevance to world models and state-persistence (Week 4 callback) |
| 1:25–1:55 | Structured discussion | Case prompts: which modern design choices are implicit answers to each problem |
| 1:55–2:00 | Synthesis | Transition to Week 12's Chinese Room debate |

### Materials/Equipment
- Slides: "Foundational Debates I: Symbol Grounding & the Frame Problem"
- Handout excerpts: Harnad (1990); McCarthy & Hayes (1969)

### Formative Check (in-class)
For a described modern system, identify one design choice that functions as an implicit (not
necessarily satisfying) answer to the frame problem, and justify the identification.
