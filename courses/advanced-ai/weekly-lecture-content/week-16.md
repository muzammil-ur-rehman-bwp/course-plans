# Week 16 — Lecture Content: Capstone Research Proposal Presentations; Course Review

## 1. The Defense Format
Each student presents their research proposal in a format modeled directly on a PhD qualifying-
exam or thesis-proposal defense, not a classroom project presentation: a focused talk (problem
statement, related work, proposed approach, feasibility argument or preliminary results,
anticipated risks and limitations) followed by committee-style questioning from the instructor
and peers. See `presentations/capstone-presentation-template.md` for the full required-slide
structure and presentation guidance, and `assignments/capstone-rubric.md` for exactly how the
defense is scored.

## 2. What Good Committee-Style Q&A Looks Like
Questions in the defense should probe exactly the same four dimensions the Week 15 peer-feedback
protocol trained: precision and genuine openness of the problem statement; accuracy and
synthesis of the related-work survey; genuine novelty of the proposed approach relative to that
survey; and honesty of the feasibility argument about its main risk. A strong defense answer to a
hard question is often "that is a real risk I have not fully resolved, and here is the strongest
counter-argument I have" — this is a better answer than an overstated, unsupported claim of
certainty, and is scored accordingly per the rubric.

## 4. Connecting to What Comes Next
This course's foundations connect directly to further work in the sibling postgraduate courses
and to an actual thesis trajectory: regret-theoretic tools (Weeks 2–4) recur in the theoretical
analysis of learning algorithms studied in *Advanced Machine Learning*; multi-agent and
game-theoretic reasoning (Weeks 5–7) recurs in any multi-component or multi-model system studied
in *Advanced Deep Learning* or *Advanced Artificial Neural Network*; the alignment and
interpretability material (Weeks 8–10) is directly relevant to any thesis topic touching deployed
or deployed-adjacent learned systems in any of the sibling courses; and the research-methods and
foundational-debates material (Weeks 11–15) is exactly the skill set a thesis proposal itself
will require, regardless of which technical area it ultimately specializes in.

## 5. Closing Discussion Prompt
In pairs or small groups: which of this course's four pillars most directly informs your own
likely thesis direction, and what is the single most important open question from that pillar
(stated in your own words) that you would want your thesis to make progress on?
