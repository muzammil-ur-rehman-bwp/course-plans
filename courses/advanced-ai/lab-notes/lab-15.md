# Lab Notes 15 — Capstone Peer-Review Workshop

**Concept recap:** feedback this week is modeled on how a thesis-proposal committee questions a
candidate — critical, not merely encouraging — and is structured around four specific questions
(precision/openness of the problem statement; accuracy and gap-motivation of the survey; genuine
novelty of the proposed approach; honesty of the feasibility argument's named risk), per Week 15,
§2.

**Common pitfalls:**
- Giving "looks good, I like the topic" feedback that does not engage any of the four questions —
  this is the Week 15 lecture content's own named example of **weak feedback**, and reviews of
  this kind cannot receive credit on Task B regardless of how much time was spent.
- Spot-checking a cited paper from memory rather than actually reopening the paper (or the
  student's own citation) during Task B, question (2) — the protocol specifically calls for
  checking the student's summary against what the paper, read directly, actually claims.
- In Task C, defensively dismissing all received critique rather than genuinely triaging it — a
  triage that disagrees with every point, with no specific counter-reasoning for each, reads as
  avoidance rather than engagement, and the task explicitly asks for reasoned agreement or
  disagreement per question.
- Writing a Task D revision plan that restates the draft's existing content rather than naming a
  concrete, specific change — "I will improve my related-work survey" is not a revision plan in
  the sense Week 15 requires; "I will add a sentence to my Auer et al. (2002) summary explicitly
  stating how its noise model differs from mine" is.

**Debugging tip:** if your Task B review does not name a specific sentence, claim, or cited paper
in the draft you are critiquing, it is not yet specific enough — generic feedback is exactly the
Weak Feedback pattern Week 15 warns against, and a review built this way will not help your peer
produce an actionable revision plan either.

**Instructor tip:** pair students with a draft outside their own exact capstone subtopic where
possible — a reviewer unfamiliar with the specific bandit-theory, mechanism-design, or alignment
niche in question is a better stand-in for the outside, skeptical committee member the Week
13–16 standard is modeling than a reviewer who already shares all the author's background
assumptions and is predisposed to agree.
