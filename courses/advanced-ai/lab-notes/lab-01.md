# Lab Notes 1 — Research Landscape Mapping & Environment Setup

**Concept recap:** this course assumes graduate AI as settled background and organizes its own
new material into four pillars (regret/bandits; multi-agent RL/game theory; AI safety/alignment/
interpretability; foundational debates/research methods).

**Common pitfalls:**
- Treating Task A's self-assessment as a formality rather than actually identifying weak spots —
  students who skip genuine review of a shaky prerequisite topic often struggle disproportionately
  in Weeks 2–7, where that background is used, not re-taught.
- In Task B, confusing "this question *mentions* a graduate-AI term" with "this question
  *belongs* to that pillar" — e.g., a question about multi-agent RL mentions Nash equilibria but
  belongs to Pillar 2 (multi-agent systems), not a restatement of the graduate course's game
  theory week.
- Writing an overly broad Task C research-interest paragraph ("I'm interested in AI safety") —
  this is exactly the vague-topic failure mode Week 13 will formally warn against; encourage even
  a rough first attempt at specificity now.

**Debugging tip:** if Task D's keyword-matching function returns no pillar for an obviously
relevant prompt, check that your keyword lists are lowercased consistently with the input text's
casing before matching.

**Instructor tip:** collect Task C's paragraphs now (ungraded) and return them to students
unchanged in Week 13, alongside the precise-problem-statement standard — the contrast between the
Week 1 and Week 13 versions of their own stated interest is an effective, low-cost way to make the
semester's intended progression visible to students themselves.
