# Lab Notes 14 — Frontier Paper Mini-Investigation

**Concept recap:** this week is explicitly flagged as covering a fast-moving research area — the
graded skill is locating, reading, and critically situating a current primary source against
this course's foundations, not memorizing any particular current result as settled fact (Week
14, header note).

**Common pitfalls:**
- Treating a paper's own "future work" section as automatically *the* most interesting open
  question, without evaluating whether a more specific or more interesting gap exists elsewhere
  in the paper — Task D asks for the student's own identification, not a transcription of the
  authors' stated future work.
- Conflating "a pillar the paper name-drops in its introduction" with "a pillar the paper
  actually builds on technically" — e.g., a paper that mentions alignment in passing without
  using any alignment-specific technique or result has not actually built on that pillar in the
  sense Task C requires; the mapping must cite a specific mechanism, not a keyword.
- In Task D, classifying a claim as "evidence-supported" without being able to point to the
  specific section, figure, or result that supports it — if you cannot name where in the paper
  the evidence lives, the claim has likely been mis-classified.
- Extracting the problem statement from the abstract's final sentence verbatim rather than
  restating it in your own words — Task B explicitly requires your own restatement, which is
  also how you demonstrate (to yourself and the grader) that you understood the question rather
  than located a quotable sentence.

**Debugging tip:** for each claim you classify as evidence-supported, write down the specific
section/figure/result before finalizing your brief — if you cannot do this for a claim, move it
to the speculative-claims list instead.

**Instructor tip:** rotate which current papers are assigned each offering (per `course-plan.md`
§9) and keep a running, anonymized collection of strong and weak student briefs as calibration
examples for future terms — this is especially useful here, since no fixed answer key is possible
for a fast-moving-area assignment the way it is for earlier weeks' quizzes.
