# Capstone Presentation Template (Student-Facing)

**Target length:** 5–7 minutes + Q&A | **Given to students in Week 11 alongside the proposal
guidelines, used for the Week 16 presentation.**

## Required Slides
1. **Title** — project name, your name(s).
2. **Problem statement** — what are you solving (a diagnostic, configuration/scheduling,
   classification, or planning problem), and why does it matter?
3. **Formalisms integrated** — name the **at least two** formalisms from this course your system
   integrates (e.g., rule-based reasoning + frame-based taxonomy; CSP solving + ontology-style
   taxonomy), and why you chose this combination for this problem.
4. **Approach** — the specific representation you built: your rule base/frame hierarchy/
   constraint model/etc., and how the two (or more) formalisms actually interact in your system.
5. **Results** — your evaluation plan from the proposal and what it shows; include at least one
   concrete worked example, derivation trace, or table of results.
6. **Demo** (if applicable) — a live or recorded demonstration of the system working (e.g., a
   query answered with its derivation trace, a configuration found, a plan produced).
7. **Limitations & next steps** — one honest limitation (e.g., a default-logic conflict your
   system doesn't resolve, a CSP that scales poorly, an inheritance case your frame hierarchy
   gets wrong) and what you'd try with more time.
8. **Questions** — invite audience questions.

## Presentation Tips
- Practice the timing — 5–7 minutes goes faster than it sounds; cut detail, not clarity.
- Lead with the problem, not the code — the audience needs to care about the "why" before the
  "how."
- Show one concrete worked example or derivation trace early; abstract descriptions without
  evidence are weak.
- Be explicit about which two (or more) formalisms you integrated and how they genuinely
  interact — a system where the second formalism is cosmetic will be questioned on this directly
  in Q&A, since it is exactly what the rubric (`assignments/capstone-rubric.md`) checks for.
- Be honest about what didn't work — the rubric explicitly rewards sound analysis over simply
  claiming success.
- Remember the scope rule from the proposal guidelines: this capstone is built on formalisms from
  Weeks 1–15, not a scikit-learn ML workflow — frame your presentation accordingly.
