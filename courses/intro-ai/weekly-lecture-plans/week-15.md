# Week 15 Lecture Plan — Introduction to AI
## Topic: Computer Vision & Robotics Overview + AI Ethics

**Duration:** 2 hours lecture + 3 hour lab

### Learning Objectives (Bloom's Level)
1. Explain the core problem of computer vision and describe edge detection as a simple concrete operation. (*Understand*)
2. Explain the sense-plan-act cycle in robotics and relate it to agents/search/planning from earlier weeks. (*Understand*)
3. Evaluate an AI system or scenario for bias, fairness, safety, and societal-impact concerns. (*Evaluate*)

### Lecture Structure
| Time | Segment | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:25 | Computer vision overview | Images as pixel arrays; the gap between pixels and meaning; edge detection as a simple operator |
| 0:25–0:50 | Robotics overview | Sensors/actuators recap (ties to Week 1 PEAS); the sense-plan-act cycle; localization and path planning (brief, connecting to Weeks 3–4 search) |
| 0:50–1:00 | Break | — |
| 1:00–1:40 | AI ethics | Bias and fairness (data bias, representational harm); safety (robustness, unintended behavior); privacy; societal impact (automation, accountability) |
| 1:40–2:00 | Case discussion | Small-group discussion of a short case study; report back to class |

### Materials/Equipment
- Slides: edge-detection before/after image, sense-plan-act diagram, ethics case study handout
- Starter notebook: simple thresholding/edge-detection demo on a small synthetic image array

### Formative Check (in-class)
Exercise: for the case-study scenario, each group identifies which ethical concern (bias,
safety, privacy, societal impact) is most relevant and why.

### Link to Lab/Assessment
Lab 15: Implement a simple image-thresholding/edge-detection demo and a simple reactive-agent
grid-world simulation; submit short written answers to the ethics case-study questions.
