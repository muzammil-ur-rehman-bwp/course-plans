# Capstone Presentation Template (Student-Facing)

**Target length:** 5–7 minutes + Q&A | **Given to students in Week 11 alongside the proposal
guidelines, used for the Week 16 presentation.**

## Required Slides
1. **Title** — project name, your name(s).
2. **Problem statement** — what are you solving (a game, a diagnostic scenario, a planning
   problem, an inference problem), and why does it matter?
3. **Classical technique** — which single classical technique from this course (search, logic,
   planning, or probabilistic reasoning) you applied, and why you chose it for this problem.
4. **Approach** — the specific domain/representation you built (e.g., the game you implemented
   minimax for, the knowledge base you built, the planning domain, or the Bayesian network
   structure).
5. **Results** — your evaluation plan from the proposal and what it shows; include at least one
   concrete worked example, trace, or table of results.
6. **Demo** (if applicable) — a live or recorded demonstration of the system working (e.g., the
   agent playing a game, the planner producing a plan, the network answering a query).
7. **Limitations & next steps** — one honest limitation (e.g., scales poorly, a simplifying
   independence assumption that doesn't quite hold) and what you'd try with more time.
8. **Questions** — invite audience questions.

## Presentation Tips
- Practice the timing — 5–7 minutes goes faster than it sounds; cut detail, not clarity.
- Lead with the problem, not the code — the audience needs to care about the "why" before the
  "how."
- Show one concrete worked example or result early; abstract descriptions without evidence are
  weak.
- Be honest about what didn't work — the rubric (`assignments/capstone-rubric.md`) explicitly
  rewards sound analysis over simply claiming success.
- Remember the scope rule from the proposal guidelines: this capstone is built on a *classical*
  technique from Weeks 3–11, not a scikit-learn ML workflow — frame your presentation
  accordingly.
