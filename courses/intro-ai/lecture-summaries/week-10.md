# Week 10 Summary — Uncertainty & Probability

**Key takeaways:**
- Logic alone cannot represent degrees of belief; probability theory fills this gap.
- The product rule (`P(A,B) = P(A|B)P(B) = P(B|A)P(A)`) rearranges directly into Bayes' rule.
- Bayes' rule combines a prior, a likelihood, and a normalizing constant into a posterior
  probability; base-rate neglect (ignoring the prior) is a common and costly reasoning error.
- Conditional independence is the assumption that will let us represent large joint
  distributions compactly next week (Bayesian networks).

**You should now be able to:** apply Bayes' rule to a diagnostic-test-style problem; explain why
a positive test on a rare condition can still leave disease probability low; check independence
numerically.

**Next week:** Bayesian networks — compact representation of a joint distribution and inference
by enumeration.
