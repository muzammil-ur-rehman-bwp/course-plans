# Quiz 5 — Neural Architecture Search and Systems-View Scaling Laws (Week 12)

**Duration:** 15 minutes | **Format:** 5 short-answer/code questions | **Weight:** part of
Quizzes (10%, best 5 of 6 counted)

1. Name the three components any NAS method is fully specified by, and give one example of each.
   (2 pts)
2. Explain, in 2–3 sentences, why NAS's own search cost has historically been a major practical
   constraint, and name one development created specifically to address it. (2 pts)
3. Write the training-FLOPs approximation $C\approx 6ND$, and state what compute-optimal
   allocation says about growing $N$ and $D$ as $C$ grows. (2 pts)
4. State, in one sentence, how this course's Week 11 scaling-laws treatment differs from the
   sibling *Advanced Artificial Neural Network* course's theoretical treatment. (2 pts)
5. A colleague claims: "Since differentiable NAS uses ordinary gradient descent, it must always
   find at least as good an architecture as evolutionary search." Identify precisely what is
   wrong with this claim. (2 pts)

**Answer key available to instructors in the course LMS gradebook (not distributed to students
before the quiz).**
