# Quiz 2 — Mixture-of-Experts and In-Context Learning Mechanics (Week 5)

**Duration:** 15 minutes | **Format:** 5 short-answer/code questions | **Weight:** part of
Quizzes (10%, best 5 of 6 counted)

1. Write the top-$k$ MoE layer's output equation, and state which quantity scales with $N$ and
   which scales with $k$. (2 pts)
2. Explain, in 2–3 sentences, why an MoE router tends to collapse onto a few popular experts
   without an explicit mitigation. (2 pts)
3. State precisely what makes in-context learning (ICL) surprising relative to the standard
   fine-tuning picture. (2 pts)
4. Describe the two-head induction-head circuit's mechanism in one or two sentences, and name one
   piece of evidence (beyond "attention looks interesting here") supporting it. (2 pts)
5. A colleague claims: "The linear-attention/linear-regression equivalence to gradient descent
   proves that ICL in large language models literally is gradient descent." Identify precisely
   what is wrong with this claim. (2 pts)

**Answer key available to instructors in the course LMS gradebook (not distributed to students
before the quiz).**
