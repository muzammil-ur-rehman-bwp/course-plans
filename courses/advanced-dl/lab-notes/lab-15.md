# Lab Notes 15 — Capstone Peer-Review Workshop

**Concept recap:** the four review questions (precise/open problem statement; survey accuracy/
synthesis; genuine novelty of approach; honest feasibility argument) structure both the peer
review and the resulting revision plan.

**Common pitfalls:**
- Giving encouraging-but-unspecific feedback ("this looks good," "maybe add more detail") instead
  of a specific sentence/section and exactly what is missing or unclear — this is the single most
  common failure mode of peer review generally, and directly undermines the exercise's purpose.
- As an author, being defensive about feedback rather than treating the workshop as a genuine dry
  run for Week 16's committee questioning — the more honestly a weakness is surfaced now, the
  cheaper it is to fix before the graded defense.
- In the revision plan, writing "will consider this" rather than committing to a specific change
  (or a specific, reasoned disagreement) — a revision plan with no concrete action items is not
  useful preparation for Week 16.
- Reviewing only for the feasibility argument (the most "exciting" component to critique) while
  neglecting the related-work survey's synthesis quality — all four review questions carry real
  weight in `assignments/capstone-rubric.md`.

**Debugging tip (for reviewers):** if you cannot articulate, in one sentence, exactly what
evidence would resolve a peer's problem statement, say so directly — that is itself the most
valuable feedback the precision test can produce.

**Instructor tip:** circulate actively during the workshop and intervene when a group's feedback
style has drifted into general encouragement rather than the specific, itemized critique the
rubric requires — this is the single highest-leverage moment to correct before Week 16's graded
defense.
