# Presentation: Module 4 — NAS, Systems-Scale Training, Research Methods, and Capstone (Weeks 10–16)

**Format:** Slide deck outline for lecture delivery (convert to slides in institution's template).

1. **Title slide** — Advanced Deep Learning (Post Graduate): Module 4, NAS, Systems-Scale
   Training, Research Methods, and Capstone
2. **The NAS problem, decomposed** — search space, search strategy, performance estimation
3. **RL-based, evolutionary, and differentiable search** — three strategies, one shared
   decomposition
4. **Why NAS's own search cost is a central constraint** — weight sharing and differentiable
   relaxation as direct responses, not independent improvements
5. **Scoping scaling laws precisely** — this course's systems/engineering question vs. the
   sibling *Advanced Artificial Neural Network* course's theoretical "why" question
6. **Compute-optimal allocation** — $C\approx 6ND$; growing $N$ and $D$ together
7. **Checkpointing and fault tolerance** — the optimal checkpoint interval; elastic restart at
   scale
8. **Frontier generative-modeling survey** — consistency-model-style ideas; progressive/step
   distillation, reusing Week 8's framing (explicitly flagged as fast-moving)
9. **Research methods for applied DL** — reading a systems paper; benchmark culture;
   reproducibility challenges specific to DL
10. **Current open problems survey** — MoE load balancing, ICL-mechanism generality, DPO's
    Bradley-Terry robustness (explicitly flagged as fast-moving, refreshed each offering)
11. **The research-proposal structure, in full** — problem statement, 5+ paper survey, proposed
    approach, feasibility argument
12. **The four review questions** — the thesis-committee-style peer-review standard used in
    Weeks 15–16
13. **Module 4 recap** — key results checklist (NAS decomposition, compute-optimal allocation,
    the research-proposal structure)
14. **Course closing** — full course map recap; connections to the sibling postgraduate courses
    and to current DL research venues

**Speaker notes:** this module shifts from derivation-heavy lecture to critical-writing and
proposal-development work from Week 12 onward — pace accordingly, and treat Weeks 15–16's content
as primarily workshop/defense time rather than new lecture material to deliver.
