# Assignment 4 — Paper Critique & Presentation (Week 15)

**Weight:** 10% of course grade | **Assigned:** Week 9 (choose topic by Week 11) | **Due:** Week 15 (written critique + in-class presentation)

## Overview
Select one real, published statistical-learning-theory paper and write a critical analysis of
its claims, assumptions, and evidence, applying the reading framework from Week 15. This
assignment is distinct from the three problem-set assignments and from the Research Capstone: it
is a close reading and critique of a *single* existing paper, not an original experiment.

## Suggested Topic Areas (Pick a Paper Yourself — Not a Closed List)
Choose any peer-reviewed or well-established preprint paper related to a topic covered in this
course, for example:
- A paper proving or extending a generalization bound (PAC, VC-dimension, or Rademacher-complexity
  based) for a specific hypothesis class.
- A paper on margin-based generalization theory for boosting or large-margin classifiers.
- A paper on reproducing kernel Hilbert space theory or the representer theorem in a specific
  application setting.
- A paper on Gaussian Process regression/classification methodology or scalability.
- A paper on Conditional Random Fields or another structured-prediction model.
- A paper on PCA variants, kernel PCA, or manifold learning methods (Isomap, t-SNE, or related).
- A paper on causal inference methodology relevant to ML (confounding adjustment, causal
  discovery, or similar).
- A paper on model-selection theory (information criteria, cross-validation theory, or related).

Students must get their chosen paper approved by the instructor by the end of Week 11 to ensure
appropriate scope and rigor.

## Deliverables
1. **Written critique (60%), 2–3 pages:** restate the paper's main claim(s) precisely; identify
   and state every assumption the claim depends on; assess whether any bound/result stated is
   informative (non-vacuous) at the scale the paper applies it; evaluate whether the paper's own
   experiments (if any) actually test the claimed generality; and give your own assessment of the
   paper's contribution and limitations.
2. **In-class presentation (40%), 8–10 minutes + Q&A:** present the same critique in talk format
   during the Week 15 research-methods session, aimed at your classmates.

## Grading Rubric
| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Accurate restatement of the paper's claim(s) and assumptions | 25% |
| Correct identification of whether/where a bound is vacuous or a claim is over-stated | 25% |
| Quality of the evidence assessment (does the paper's own evidence support its claim at the stated generality?) | 20% |
| Written clarity and organization | 15% |
| Presentation clarity and quality of Q&A responses | 15% |

## Submission
Submit the written critique as `paper_critique.pdf` via the course submission system by the start
of Week 15; present in class during Week 15.
