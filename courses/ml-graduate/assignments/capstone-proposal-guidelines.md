# Research Capstone — Proposal Guidelines

**Due:** Week 9–10 | **Weight:** part of the Research Capstone grade (25% total course weight)

## What to Submit
A 1–2 page proposal (`capstone_proposal.pdf` or `.md`) covering:
1. **Subtopic:** which statistical-learning-theory subtopic from this course you are pursuing
   (e.g., tightening a generalization bound for a specific class; extending GP regression to a
   new kernel; a deeper empirical study of margin theory for boosting; a causal-inference
   extension; an information-criterion comparison study) — must connect clearly to course content.
2. **Literature review scope:** the 3–5 papers you plan to review, with one sentence each on why
   each is relevant (full review is written later; this is a scoping list).
3. **Planned experiment or derivation:** what you will reproduce or extend — a specific
   experiment, a specific derivation extension, or both. State clearly what would count as
   "done."
4. **Evaluation/validation plan:** how you will check your reproduced result or derivation is
   correct (e.g., matching a known closed-form special case, cross-checking against a reference
   implementation, a sanity-check limiting case).
5. **Team:** individual or pair, with each member's planned contribution if a pair.

## Approval
The instructor will respond within one week with approval or requested revisions. Projects must
connect to a statistical-learning-theory topic covered in this course; topics drawn primarily
from neural-network-specific theory, deep architectures, or general PGM-inference/MDP topics
(owned by sibling graduate courses) require instructor pre-approval and strong justification for
why this course's theory is still the central lens.

## Example Topics (For Inspiration, Not a Closed List)
Extending the VC-dimension analysis to a new hypothesis class; a tighter margin-based bound for a
specific weak-learner family; a from-scratch sparse/variational GP approximation and its accuracy
tradeoffs; a CRF feature-engineering case study with an ablation; a comparative empirical study of
AIC/BIC/cross-validation on a real dataset; an applied causal-inference case study using a public
confounded dataset.
