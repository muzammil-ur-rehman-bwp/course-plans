# Capstone Presentation Template (Student-Facing)

**Target length:** 8–10 minutes + Q&A | **Given to students in Week 9 alongside the proposal
guidelines, used for the Week 16 presentation.**

## Required Slides
1. **Title** — project title, your name(s), chosen statistical-learning-theory subtopic.
2. **Motivation** — why this subtopic matters and what question you set out to answer.
3. **Related work** — your 3–5 reviewed papers, compared to one another (not just listed), and
   where your project sits relative to them.
4. **Method** — the specific experiment you reproduced/extended, or the derivation you worked
   through, stated precisely (assumptions included).
5. **Results** — your findings, with at least one chart/table/derivation result shown explicitly.
6. **Validation** — how you checked correctness (e.g., matching a known special case, a reference
   implementation, a sanity-check limit).
7. **Limitations & next steps** — one honest limitation and what you would try with more time.
8. **Questions** — invite audience questions.

## Presentation Tips
- Practice the timing — 8–10 minutes goes faster than it sounds; cut detail, not clarity.
- Lead with the question your project answers, not the mechanics of the derivation or code — the
  audience needs to care about the "why" before the "how."
- Show one concrete number, plot, or derived result early; abstract descriptions without evidence
  are weak, especially for a theory-focused audience.
- Be honest about what didn't work or didn't match expectations — the rubric
  (`assignments/capstone-rubric.md`) explicitly rewards sound analysis over simply claiming
  success.
- Treat Q&A like a conference talk: if you don't know an answer, say so and state how you would
  find out, rather than guessing.
