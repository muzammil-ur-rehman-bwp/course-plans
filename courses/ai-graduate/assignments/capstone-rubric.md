# Research Capstone — Grading Rubric

**Due:** Draft paper Week 15; final paper + presentation Week 16 | **Weight:** 25% of course
grade total (includes proposal, literature review, experiment, paper, and presentation)

| Component | Weight (of 25%) | Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| Proposal | 2% | Specific motivating question, genuine candidate literature, feasible and concretely scoped experiment plan |
| Literature Review | 5% | 3–5 relevant papers accurately summarized (problem, method, result); ends with an explicit stated gap/question the experiment addresses, not just a list of summaries |
| Experiment Design & Execution | 6% | Appropriate benchmark/task; fair, clearly stated baseline; ablation where applicable; multiple random seeds reported with spread for any stochastic method (per Week 13) |
| Results & Analysis | 5% | Results reported honestly, including negative/partial results; analysis correctly interprets what the results do and do not show |
| Written Paper | 4% | Clear conference-short-paper structure (introduction, related work, method, results, limitations); properly cited sources |
| Presentation | 3% | Clear conference-style talk within time; demo/results shown where applicable; accurate, honest Q&A responses |

## Grading Notes
- This is a **research-style** rubric: a project that honestly reports a negative or partial
  result, correctly analyzed against a sound experiment design, can score as well as — or better
  than — a project with impressive-looking results but a weak literature review, no baseline, or
  single-run "evidence." The soundness of the literature review and experimental design matters
  as much as the raw outcome.
- Reproducing a published result (even approximately, at smaller scale) is explicitly encouraged
  and does not count against originality; presenting someone else's reported numbers as your own
  experimental findings, without actually running the experiment, is an academic integrity
  violation (see `course-plan.md` §10).
- Pairs must clearly attribute each member's contribution in the written paper; grading can
  differ between partners if contributions were significantly uneven.
