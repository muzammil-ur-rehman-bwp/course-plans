# Paper Critique & Presentation Assignment (Week 13–15)

**Weight:** 10% of course grade | **Assigned:** Week 13 | **Due:** Written critique by Week 15;
in-class presentation during Week 15 seminar sessions

## Purpose
This assignment is the dedicated, graded application of Week 13's research-methods skills: read
a real AI research paper, critique it using the structured framework from Week 13, and present
your critique to the class. It is distinct from, and in addition to, the Research Capstone — the
capstone involves a literature review and an experiment on a topic of the student's choosing;
this assignment is a focused critique of a single paper.

## Choosing a Paper
Select one paper related to any topic covered in this course (advanced search, game theory,
automated reasoning, planning, MDPs/RL, POMDPs, probabilistic inference, or one of the Week 14
current-trends areas) from a current AAAI, IJCAI, or NeurIPS proceedings, or from the instructor-
provided suggested reading list distributed in Week 13. The paper must be a real, publicly
available paper you can cite properly (title, authors, venue, year) in your submission — do not
submit a critique of a paper you cannot actually produce a citation for.

## Deliverable 1: Written Critique (approx. 500–800 words)
Using the Week 13 structured-critique framework, address each of the following explicitly:
1. **Claim:** state the paper's central claim precisely.
2. **Evidence:** summarize the evidence offered and whether it actually supports the claim.
3. **Baselines:** assess whether the baselines compared against are fair and current for the
   paper's publication venue/year.
4. **Ablations:** does the paper isolate which part of its method drives the improvement?
5. **Reproducibility:** identify at least one concrete reproducibility concern (or note that the
   paper does unusually well on this front, with specifics).
6. **Your overall assessment:** in 2–3 sentences, state whether you find the paper's central
   claim convincing, and why.

## Deliverable 2: In-Class Presentation (5–7 minutes + Q&A, during Week 15)
Present your critique to the class in the same structure as the written critique. You will field
questions from classmates and the instructor; answering accurately and honestly (including "I'm
not sure, the paper doesn't say" where true) is part of the graded presentation skill.

## Grading Rubric
| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Claim and evidence accurately and precisely identified | 15 |
| Baseline and ablation assessment, specific and well-reasoned | 20 |
| Reproducibility concern(s) identified, specific (not generic) | 15 |
| Overall assessment is a defensible, non-overstated judgment | 15 |
| Presentation: clear, within time, handles Q&A accurately | 25 |
| Proper citation of the chosen paper | 10 |
| **Total** | **100** |

## Notes
- This assignment is graded on the quality of critique, not on whether the chosen paper turns
  out to be "good" or "bad" — a well-reasoned critique of a strong paper (identifying its real
  strengths precisely) scores as well as a well-reasoned critique of a flawed one.
