# Lab Notes 15 — Guided Capstone Work Session

**Concept recap:** this session applies Week 13's research-methods checklist (literature review
with a stated gap; benchmark/baseline/ablation/multiple-seeds experiment design) directly to
each student's own capstone project, with structured peer feedback as a rehearsal for Week 16.

**Common pitfalls:**
- Treating this session as unstructured free time rather than using the specific checkpoints
  (Tasks A–D) — the most common failure mode is spending the whole session on experiment code
  with no time left for the literature-review gap check or the peer-feedback exchange, both of
  which directly affect the Week 16 grade.
- Giving peer feedback that is encouraging but non-specific ("sounds good!") — per Week 15's
  lecture content, feedback must be specific and actionable on each of the three named axes.
- Waiting until this session to discover a fundamental problem with the experiment design (e.g.,
  no baseline, no plan for multiple seeds) — ideally these were caught during Week 7–8's topic
  selection or Week 13's methods week; if discovered only now, prioritize fixing the design over
  running more of a flawed experiment.
- Writing the Task D work-session note generically after the fact rather than during/immediately
  after the session — specific notes (what feedback was received, what will change) are far more
  useful than a vague summary for tracking progress toward Week 16.

**Debugging tip:** if your experiment's results look "too good" or "too clean," re-check for a
data leak or an unintentionally favorable comparison (e.g., comparing against a weaker baseline
than the literature review identified) before trusting the result.

**Instructor tip:** circulate actively during this session rather than waiting for students to
ask for help — many capstone problems (an unstated gap, a missing baseline, an unplanned
single-seed experiment) are easier to catch by proactively glancing at a student's draft than by
waiting for them to self-report being stuck.
