# Week 13 Lecture Plan — Artificial Intelligence (Graduate)
## Topic: Research Methods in AI

**Duration:** 2 hours lecture + 3 hour lab/seminar

### Learning Objectives (Bloom's Level)
1. Apply an efficient strategy for reading a research paper (abstract → figures/results →
   methods → related work → full read). (*Apply*)
2. Critique a paper's claims, evidence, baselines, and reproducibility. (*Analyze, Evaluate*)
3. Design a sound small experiment, including baselines, ablations, and attention to
   statistical significance. (*Create, Evaluate*)

### Lecture Structure
| Time | Segment | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | How to read a paper | The abstract-first, figures-second reading strategy |
| 0:20–0:50 | How to critique a paper | Claim vs. evidence; baseline adequacy; structured critique walkthrough |
| 0:50–1:15 | Reproducibility | Common reproducibility failures in AI research (missing hyperparameters, seeds, compute) |
| 1:15–1:50 | Experimental design | Benchmarks, baselines, ablation studies, statistical significance (multiple seeds) |
| 1:50–2:00 | Assignment launch | Paper Critique & Presentation assignment explained |

### Materials/Equipment
- Slides: "Reading, Critiquing, and Designing AI Research"
- Handout: paper-critique structured worksheet
- Suggested reading list (instructor-curated, refreshed each term)

### Formative Check (in-class)
Given a short paper excerpt, identify the central claim, the evidence offered for it, and at
least one reproducibility concern, using the structured worksheet.

### Link to Lab/Assessment
Lab 13: apply the structured critique worksheet to a short paper excerpt and draft an ablation
study design for a hypothetical system (see `lab-manuals/lab-13.md`). **Paper Critique &
Presentation assignment assigned** (see `assignments/paper-critique-and-presentation.md`; due
Week 15).
