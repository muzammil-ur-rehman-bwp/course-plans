# Week 4 Summary — Game Theory II & Multi-Agent Systems

**Key takeaways:**
- Multi-agent systems are classified along two axes: cooperative vs. competitive, and zero-sum
  vs. general-sum; coordination problems can arise even among purely cooperative agents.
- Mechanism design flips classical game theory: given a desired outcome, design rules so that
  self-interested equilibrium behavior achieves it.
- In a second-price (Vickrey) auction, bidding one's true valuation is a dominant strategy — a
  result with a clean case-by-case argument, and the reason such mechanisms are used in real
  resource-allocation and ad-auction systems.
- This week's game theory is the lens Week 14 reuses to understand multi-agent reinforcement
  learning.

**You should now be able to:** classify a multi-agent scenario along both axes; explain why
truthful bidding is a dominant strategy in a second-price auction; simulate a simple auction in
Python.
