# Paper Critique & Presentation Assignment (Week 13–15)

**Weight:** 10% of course grade | **Assigned:** Week 13 | **Due:** Written critique by Week 15;
in-class presentation during the Week 15 research seminar session

## Purpose
This assignment is the dedicated, graded application of Week 13's research-methods skills: read
a real, current KR research paper, critique it using the structured framework introduced that
week, and present your critique to the class. It is distinct from, and in addition to, the
Research Proposal Capstone — the capstone formulates and scopes the student's own original
research question; this assignment critiques a single existing paper written by someone else.

## Choosing a Paper
Select one real, publicly available paper from a current KR conference, an IJCAI/AAAI KR track,
or the journal *Artificial Intelligence* (or an equivalent current venue), related to any pillar
covered in this course: expressive logics (HOL/type theory, many-valued/paraconsistent logics,
SROIQ — Weeks 2–4), structured/quantitative non-classical reasoning (ASPIC+, probabilistic logic
programming — Weeks 5–6), neuro-symbolic integration and formal verification (Weeks 7–9), or
multi-agent/explanatory/methodological topics (belief merging, explanation research, ontology
evolution — Weeks 10–12), or one of the current directions surveyed in Week 14. A list of
suggested topic areas and venues is distributed by the instructor in Week 13; students may also
propose their own paper with instructor approval. The paper must be one you can properly cite
(title, authors, venue, year) in your submission — do not submit a critique of a paper you cannot
actually produce a citation for.

## Deliverable 1: Written Critique (approx. 500–800 words)
Using the Week 13 structured-critique framework, address each of the following explicitly:
1. **Claim:** state the paper's central claim precisely.
2. **Evidence:** summarize the evidence offered and whether it actually supports the claim.
3. **Formal rigor:** assess whether the paper's claimed formal property (soundness, completeness,
   a complexity bound, decidability) is actually proved, or only conjectured/asserted.
4. **Reproducibility:** identify at least one concrete reproducibility concern (or note that the
   paper does unusually well on this front, with specifics) — is the formalism's semantics fully
   and unambiguously specified; is example code or a reference encoding available.
5. **Course-pillar connection:** state which of this course's pillars the paper builds on and
   how, and name one specific question the paper leaves open — direct rehearsal of the Week 13/14
   problem-statement-extraction skill, as preparation for your own capstone.
6. **Your overall assessment:** in 2–3 sentences, state whether you find the paper's central
   claim convincing, and why.

## Deliverable 2: In-Class Presentation (5–7 minutes + Q&A, during Week 15)
Present your critique to the class in the same structure as the written critique. You will field
questions from classmates and the instructor; answering accurately and honestly (including "I'm
not sure, the paper doesn't say" where true) is part of the graded presentation skill.

## Grading Rubric
| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Claim and evidence accurately and precisely identified | 15 |
| Formal-rigor assessment, specific and well-reasoned | 15 |
| Reproducibility concern(s) identified, specific (not generic) | 15 |
| Course-pillar connection and open question, specific and accurate | 15 |
| Overall assessment is a defensible, non-overstated judgment | 10 |
| Presentation: clear, within time, handles Q&A accurately | 20 |
| Proper citation of the chosen paper | 10 |
| **Total** | **100** |

## Notes
- This assignment is graded on the quality of critique, not on whether the chosen paper turns
  out to be "good" or "bad" — a well-reasoned critique of a strong paper (identifying its real
  strengths precisely) scores as well as a well-reasoned critique of a flawed one.
- Presenting a paper's reported results as more conclusive than the paper itself claims, or
  omitting a reproducibility concern you are aware of, is handled as an academic-integrity matter
  per `course-plan.md` §10 if it rises to misrepresentation, not merely a grading deduction.
