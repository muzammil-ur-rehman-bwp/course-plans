# Presentation: Module 4 — Belief Merging, Explanation, Research Methods & Capstone (Weeks 10–16)

**Format:** Slide deck outline for lecture delivery (convert to slides in institution's template).

1. **Title slide** — Advanced Knowledge Representation and Reasoning (Post Graduate): Module 4,
   Belief Merging, Explanation, Research Methods & Capstone
2. **From AGM revision to belief merging** — one privileged belief set vs. a profile of peers
3. **Distance-based merging operators** — aggregate distance, integrity constraints, the merging
   postulates (IC0–IC3)
4. **Merging vs. revision** — the structural distinction, worked on parallel examples
5. **Why ontology explanation is a research problem** — justifications, minimality, multiple
   distinct justifications
6. **Black-box vs. glass-box justification finding** — tradeoffs, and why scaling both remains
   open
7. **Proof trees vs. the full justification set** — two different explanation notions
8. **Ontology evolution and versioning** — change detection, impact analysis, backward
   compatibility, modularity's partial mitigation
9. **Research methods** — the structured critique framework; the problem-statement precision
   test; related-work survey standards
10. **Current open problems survey** — expressive-reasoning scalability; probabilistic/
    paraconsistent integration; verified neuro-symbolic systems
11. **Research proposal work session** — the committee-style peer-review workshop format
12. **Course map recap** — the full 16-week progression as one connected argument
13. **Capstone defense format** — problem statement, survey, approach, feasibility, risks,
    committee Q&A (see `capstone-presentation-template.md`)
14. **Closing: where KR research is headed** — the three Week 14 threads, revisited as the
    course's deliberately unresolved final word

**Speaker notes:** Module 4 is where the semester's research-methods thread (introduced in Week 1,
developed through Weeks 13–15) pays off directly in the Week 16 defenses — when delivering this
module's final slides, explicitly tie each capstone pillar back to the specific week it was
scoped from, so students see their own proposal as a direct continuation of the course rather
than a separate, bolted-on requirement.
