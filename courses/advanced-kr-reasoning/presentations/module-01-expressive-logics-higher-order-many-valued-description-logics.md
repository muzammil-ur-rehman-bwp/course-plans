# Presentation: Module 1 — Expressive Logics: Higher-Order, Many-Valued & Description Logics (Weeks 1–4)

**Format:** Slide deck outline for lecture delivery (convert to slides in institution's template).

1. **Title slide** — Advanced Knowledge Representation and Reasoning (Post Graduate): Module 1,
   Expressive Logics
2. **Course scope recap** — this course's four pillars vs. the graduate-KR&R prerequisite and the
   sibling postgraduate courses (AI/ANN/ML/DL, Advanced)
3. **Scoping a research proposal, previewed early** — one-slide sketch of problem
   statement/survey/approach/feasibility argument, full treatment in Week 13
4. **Why FOL has expressive limits** — quantifying over predicates; the single-sentence induction
   principle second-order logic can state and FOL cannot
5. **The simply-typed lambda calculus** — base types, function types, abstraction, application,
   β-reduction
6. **Typed KR connections** — compositional semantics and typed feature structures, surveyed
   conceptually
7. **Two-valued logic's limits** — genuine incompleteness vs. genuine inconsistency as two
   distinct problems
8. **Kleene K3 vs. Łukasiewicz Ł3** — shared ∧/∨/¬ tables; the diverging implication table on
   `U→U`
9. **Paraconsistency and FDE** — the four-valued N/T/F/B system; non-explosion demonstrated
10. **From ALC to SROIQ** — role hierarchies, complex role inclusions, number restrictions,
    nominals
11. **Extending the tableau** — the ≥-rule, the ≤-rule and merging, role-hierarchy propagation
12. **SROIQ's complexity picture** — N2ExpTime-completeness, and what specifically drives it
    beyond SHIQ's EXPTIME
13. **Module 1 recap** — key results checklist (HOL's expressive gain, K3/Ł3 divergence, SROIQ's
    constructs and complexity)
14. **Looking ahead** — "Next: structured argumentation (ASPIC+) and probabilistic logic
    programming" teaser slide

**Speaker notes:** this module's hardest conceptual pivot for students is accepting that SROIQ's
double-exponential worst case is a real, named, defensible result and not a hand-wavy "it's
harder" claim — budget real time connecting the specific constructs (nominals + inverse
roles/number restrictions) to the specific complexity jump, since Week 11's justification-finding
material later depends on students taking SROIQ's real-world reasoning cost seriously.
