# Lab Manual 13 — Structured Paper Critique and Related-Work Annotation

**Duration:** 3 hours | **Prerequisite:** Week 13 lecture

**Format note:** this is a non-code, critical-writing lab — there is no notebook/implementation
component this week.

## Objectives
Apply the structured critique framework to a short frontier-KR paper excerpt; draft and
pressure-test a capstone problem statement; annotate 5+ candidate related-work papers.

## Setup
1. Obtain the instructor-provided paper excerpt for the in-class critique exercise.
2. Begin a running document (`capstone_notes.md` or similar) you will extend through Week 15.

## Procedure
1. **Task A — Structured critique:** for the provided excerpt, state (i) the paper's central
   claim precisely; (ii) whether the excerpt's argument, as given, actually establishes it; (iii)
   one concrete reproducibility concern; (iv) which course topic the excerpt most directly builds
   on or conflicts with.
2. **Task B — Problem-statement draft:** draft a candidate capstone problem statement connected
   to one of this course's four pillars.
3. **Task C — Precision-test peer review:** exchange draft problem statements with a partner;
   each partner states, from the statement alone, what evidence would resolve it. Revise your
   statement until your partner can do this without needing clarification.
4. **Task D — Related-work annotation:** identify and annotate at least 5 candidate related-work
   papers for your capstone direction, each annotation stating the paper's precise claim and
   finding (not a paraphrase of its abstract).

## Expected Output
A written document containing Tasks A–D, with the Task C revision history shown (original
statement, partner feedback, revised statement).

## Submission
Submit your running capstone-notes document via the course submission system by the end of the
lab session.
