# Lab Manual 14 — Open-Problem Connection Essay

**Duration:** 3 hours | **Prerequisite:** Week 14 lecture

**Format note:** this is a non-code, critical-writing lab — there is no notebook/implementation
component this week.

## Objectives
State a currently open KR research problem's precise technical obstacle, identify a partial
approach's limitation, and connect the open problem to your own capstone's scope.

## Setup
1. Review the Week 14 lecture content and the instructor-provided current papers for this
   offering.
2. Continue your running capstone-notes document from Lab 13.

## Procedure
1. **Task A — Obstacle restatement:** choose one of the Week 14 survey's three open problems (or
   an instructor-approved alternative from the current reading) and state its precise technical
   obstacle **in your own words** (not a copy of the lecture content).
2. **Task B — Additional partial approach:** name one partial approach to this problem not
   already named in the Week 14 lecture content, drawn from the instructor-provided current
   reading, and state its specific limitation.
3. **Task C — Capstone scope check:** write 3–5 sentences connecting this open problem to your
   own capstone direction (from Lab 13), explicitly assessing whether your current problem
   statement sits at an appropriately specific scope — neither a restatement of the full open
   problem, nor something already resolved by material covered in this course.
4. **Task D — Scope revision (if needed):** if Task C reveals a scope issue, revise your problem
   statement accordingly and explain the change in 2–3 sentences; if no revision is needed,
   explain in 2–3 sentences why your current scope already stands up to this check.

## Expected Output
A written document containing Tasks A–D, added to your running capstone-notes document.

## Submission
Submit your updated capstone-notes document via the course submission system by the end of the
lab session.
