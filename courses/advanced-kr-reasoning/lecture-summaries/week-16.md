# Week 16 Summary — Capstone Presentations; Course Review

**Key takeaways:**
- Each student defends their research proposal in a qualifying-exam/thesis-proposal-defense
  format, judged on the soundness of a plan for research not yet completed.
- The course map runs expressive logics → structured/quantitative non-classical reasoning →
  neuro-symbolic integration/formal verification → multi-agent/explanatory/methodological
  topics, each pillar a direct extension of a specific graduate-course foundation.
- The course closes on the Week 14 open-problems survey deliberately unresolved: expressive-
  reasoning scalability, uncertainty-plus-inconsistency integration, and verified neuro-symbolic
  systems remain open as of this offering.

**You should now be able to:** present and defend an original research proposal under
committee-style questioning; situate this course's full content within the graduate foundations
it builds on and the open-problems landscape it points toward.

**Course complete.** Final Exam (Week 17) is comprehensive, emphasizing Weeks 9–14.
