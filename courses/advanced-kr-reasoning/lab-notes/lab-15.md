# Lab Notes 15 — Proposal Workshop and Revision Plan

**Concept recap:** the Week 15 workshop re-applies the precision test, the survey-synthesis
standard, and the feasibility argument's named-risk requirement, through committee-style peer
review across two independent rotations, producing a revision plan that commits to specific
responses rather than restating the feedback.

**Common pitfalls:**
- As a reviewer, giving only encouragement-style feedback ("looks good," "I liked this part")
  instead of the specific, pointed questions Task A requires — this wastes the presenter's
  limited workshop time and does not prepare them for the real Week 16 committee.
- As a presenter, writing a Task D revision plan that merely restates the feedback received
  ("reviewer said my survey needs more synthesis") without committing to a specific change — the
  deliverable requires either a concrete planned edit or a specific, defensible reason for
  declining it.
- Treating both rotations' feedback as equally weighted even when they conflict — if two
  reviewers give contradictory advice (e.g., one says narrow the survey, one says broaden it),
  the revision plan must explicitly address the conflict and state which judgment call was made
  and why, not silently pick one without acknowledging the other.

**Debugging tip (for writing, not code):** before the session, re-run the Week 13 precision test
and the Week 14 scope check on your own draft one more time — fixing an obviously-vague problem
statement before the workshop frees your reviewers' limited time for deeper issues instead of the
basics.

**Instructor tip:** circulate between rotations and spot-check that reviewers are actually asking
pointed questions (per Task A) rather than defaulting to polite feedback — this is the single
most common way this workshop under-delivers relative to its design intent.
