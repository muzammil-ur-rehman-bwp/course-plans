# Week 11 Lecture Plan — Advanced Knowledge Representation and Reasoning (Post Graduate)
## Topic: Explanation and Justification Research

**Duration:** 2 hours lecture + 3 hour research seminar/lab

### Learning Objectives (Bloom's Level)
1. Define a justification for an entailment and explain why minimality matters. (*Understand*)
2. Construct and verify multiple distinct justifications for a toy entailment by hand. (*Apply,
   Analyze*)
3. Critically distinguish "a proof tree" from "the set of all justifications" as explanation
   notions. (*Analyze, Evaluate*)

### Lecture Structure
| Time | Segment | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Motivation | Why re-reading a tableau trace is not a human-usable explanation |
| 0:20–0:50 | Justifications | Minimal entailing subsets; multiple distinct justifications |
| 0:50–1:20 | Finding justifications | Black-box vs. glass-box approaches (conceptual) |
| 1:20–1:45 | Proof trees revisited | One derivation vs. all minimal reasons |
| 1:45–2:00 | Open-problem framing | Why this remains active KR research |

### Materials/Equipment
- Slides: "Explanation & Justification for Expressive Ontologies"
- Jupyter for the black-box justification finder

### Formative Check (in-class)
Given a toy ontology with two distinct justifications for one entailment, verify each is minimal
by removing one axiom at a time and checking the entailment breaks.

### Link to Lab/Assessment
Lab 11: implement a black-box justification finder over a toy ontology fragment (see
`lab-manuals/lab-11.md`). **Quiz 5** (Week 10 content) this week.
