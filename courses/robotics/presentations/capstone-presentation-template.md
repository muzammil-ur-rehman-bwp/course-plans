# Capstone Presentation Template (Student-Facing)

**Target length:** 5–7 minutes + Q&A | **Given to students in Week 10 alongside the proposal
guidelines, used for the Week 16 presentation.**

## Required Slides
1. **Title** — project name, your name(s).
2. **Behavior statement** — what autonomous behavior does your robot perform, and why does it
   matter?
3. **Simulation setup** — simulator, robot model, world/obstacle layout (1–2 sentences + a
   screenshot).
4. **Approach/pipeline** — which course techniques you integrated (e.g., kinematics + PID +
   path planning) and a simple pipeline diagram.
5. **Results** — your success metric(s) and what they show; include at least one chart/plot.
6. **Live or recorded demo** — the robot performing the behavior in simulation.
7. **Limitations & next steps** — one honest limitation and what you'd try with more time/data
   (or real hardware).
8. **Questions** — invite audience questions.

## Presentation Tips
- Practice the timing — 5–7 minutes goes faster than it sounds; cut detail, not clarity.
- Lead with the behavior/problem, not the code — the audience needs to see what the robot does
  before caring about how.
- The demo is the centerpiece — if it's recorded, make sure the recording is cued up and tested
  beforehand, not something you're troubleshooting live.
- Be honest about what didn't fully work — the rubric (`assignments/capstone-rubric.md`)
  explicitly rewards sound analysis over simply claiming success.
