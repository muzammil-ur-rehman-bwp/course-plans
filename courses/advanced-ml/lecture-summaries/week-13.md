# Week 13 Summary — Research Methods for Statistical Learning Theory at the Postgraduate Level

**Key takeaways:**
- Reading a theory paper means identifying its precise claimed result (upper/lower bound, or
  matching rates), its assumptions, and whether the evidence matches the stated generality.
- A problem statement must pass the precision test: a knowledgeable reader should be able to state
  what evidence would resolve it, after reading the statement alone.
- A related-work survey must accurately represent, relate, and use 5+ papers to motivate a
  specific gap; a feasibility argument must name the main risk honestly, not merely list reasons
  for optimism.
- Postgraduate work is judged on the soundness of a research plan, not on whether a fixed
  experiment was executed — a genuine departure from the graduate course's capstone standard.

**You should now be able to:** apply the precision test to a problem statement; apply the
three-part related-work survey standard; construct a rigorous feasibility argument.
