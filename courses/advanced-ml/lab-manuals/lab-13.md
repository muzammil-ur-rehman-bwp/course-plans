# Lab Manual 13 — Problem-Statement Drafting & Related-Work Annotation

**Duration:** 3 hours (workshop format) | **Prerequisite:** Week 13 lecture

## Objectives
Draft a precise, falsifiable problem statement for your capstone candidate topic, subject it to
structured peer critique using the Week 13 precision test, revise it, and annotate 5+ candidate
related-work papers using the three-part postgraduate survey standard.

## Setup
No code environment is required this week. Bring a list of 5+ candidate papers for your capstone
topic (title/authors/venue/year), identified from your own search or from instructor-suggested
venues (NeurIPS/ICML/COLT-style). Create a single document `lab13_proposal_workshop.md`.

## Procedure
1. **Task A — Problem-statement draft:** draft a one-paragraph, falsifiable problem statement for
   your own capstone candidate topic, connected to one of this course's five pillars.
2. **Task B — Precision test & peer critique:** exchange your draft with a peer. Each of you
   applies the Week 13 precision test to the other's statement (could a knowledgeable reader
   state, after reading only this statement, what evidence would resolve it? is it already
   settled by material from this course or the student's own planned survey?) and records the
   written critique received, verbatim.
3. **Task C — Revision:** revise your problem statement in direct response to the critique
   received. In 2–3 sentences, state specifically what changed and why.
4. **Task D — Related-work annotation:** for 5+ candidate papers for your capstone's
   related-work survey, annotate each using the three-part standard from Week 13, §3: what the
   paper claims, what it found, and how it relates to your revised (Task C) problem statement's
   stated gap.

## Expected Output
A single document containing Tasks A–D as clearly labeled sections, including the peer critique
received verbatim in Task B and 5+ paper annotations in Task D.

## Submission
Submit `lab13_proposal_workshop.md` via the course submission system by the end of the lab
session.
