# Week 15 — Lecture Content: Research Proposal Work Session

## 1. Purpose of This Week
This week is dedicated, structured class time for drafting and refining the capstone's four
required components (problem statement, 5+ paper related-work survey, proposed novel approach,
feasibility argument or preliminary results), building directly on the Week 13 methodology and
Week 14's open-problems calibration. There is no new theoretical content this week — the lecture
period itself is restructured as a workshop.

## 2. The Thesis-Committee-Style Peer-Feedback Protocol
Applied to a **full draft proposal** (not just the problem statement, as in Week 13's narrower
peer-critique exercise), each reviewer answers four questions, each with a specific justification
(not a rating):
1. **Is the problem statement precise and genuinely open?** Apply the Week 13 precision test, and
   check it is not already settled by material covered in this course or by the student's own
   planned survey.
2. **Does the related-work survey correctly represent and motivate from the cited papers?**
   Spot-check at least one cited paper's summary against the paper itself; check the survey
   relates papers to each other and ends by motivating the stated gap (Week 13's three-part
   standard).
3. **Is the proposed approach genuinely novel relative to the surveyed work?** Check it is the
   student's own formulation, clearly distinguishable from restating a single surveyed paper's
   contribution.
4. **Is the feasibility argument honest about the main risk?** Check it names, explicitly, the
   single most likely way the approach could fail, and gives a reasoned (not merely optimistic)
   case for why that risk is manageable (Week 13, §4's standard).

## 3. Receiving and Triaging Feedback
As an author receiving feedback, for each of the four questions: state explicitly whether you
agree with the critique received and why. Disagreement is legitimate (a reviewer may
misunderstand a technical point), but must be argued, not simply asserted — the same standard this
course has applied to every derivation all semester.

## 4. Producing a Revision Plan
For each weakness identified (by a reviewer, or by your own triage in §3), state the **specific
change** you will make before the Week 15 draft deadline (or, if this workshop occurs before that
deadline, before the final Week 16 submission) — not a vague intention ("I'll improve the
survey") but an action ("I will replace paper 3 in my survey, which I now realize does not
actually address my stated gap, with [a more relevant paper], and rewrite the survey's closing
paragraph to state the gap in terms of the statistical–computational tradeoff framing from Week
14 rather than the vaguer framing I used in the draft").

## 5. In-Class/Lab Exercise
See `lab-manuals/lab-15.md`: exchange your current capstone proposal draft with an assigned peer,
apply the §2 four-question protocol to their draft in writing, receive and triage the feedback on
your own draft (§3), and produce an explicit revision plan (§4) before the Week 15 draft deadline.
