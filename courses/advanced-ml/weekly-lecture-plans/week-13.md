# Week 13 Lecture Plan — Advanced Machine Learning (Post Graduate)
## Topic: Research Methods for Statistical Learning Theory at the Postgraduate Level

**Duration:** 2 hours lecture + 3 hour research seminar/lab (workshop format)

### Learning Objectives (Bloom's Level)
1. State the precision test for a research problem statement. (*Understand, Apply*)
2. Apply the three related-work survey standards to a candidate reading list. (*Apply, Analyze*)
3. Construct a rigorous feasibility argument. (*Analyze, Evaluate*)

### Lecture Structure
| Time | Segment | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:25 | Reading theory papers | Identifying the precise claimed result (upper/lower bound, matching rates); assumption-checking |
| 0:25–0:50 | The problem-statement precision test | Topic vs. question; worked vague/precise contrast |
| 0:50–1:20 | Related-work survey standards | Accurate representation; relating papers; motivating a stated gap |
| 1:20–1:45 | The feasibility argument | What must be true; naming the main risk honestly; arguing it is manageable |
| 1:45–2:00 | Postgraduate evaluation standard | Plan-quality vs. completed-project standard, contrasted with the graduate course's capstone |

### Materials/Equipment
- Slides: "Research Methods for Statistical Learning Theory"
- Capstone problem-statement draft template
- Candidate-paper annotation worksheet

### Formative Check (in-class)
Apply the precision test to the statement "I want to study robust statistics" versus "Does
median-of-means remain $O(\sigma\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/n})$-accurate under a dependent (non-i.i.d.,
mixing) sampling process?" and explain which is a problem statement and why.

### Link to Lab/Assessment
Lab 13: draft a problem statement, subject it to peer critique via the precision test, and
annotate 5+ candidate related-work papers (see `lab-manuals/lab-13.md`).

### Capstone Milestone
Research-methods workshop: problem-statement drafting and related-work annotation.
