# Capstone Presentation Template (Student-Facing)

**Target length:** conference-talk format, 7–10 minutes + Q&A | **Given to students in Week 9
alongside the proposal guidelines, used for the Week 16 presentation.**

## Required Slides
1. **Title** — project name, your name(s).
2. **Problem / motivating question** — the specific, concrete question your capstone
   investigates, and why it matters.
3. **Related work** — the 3–5 papers from your literature review, and the gap or question they
   leave open.
4. **Method / experiment design** — what you actually ran (reproduced or extended), and why; the
   baseline/comparison and what was held fixed.
5. **Results** — reported honestly, including negative/partial results; multiple-seed spread
   shown for any stochastic method.
6. **Analysis** — what the results do and do not show; does the evidence support your motivating
   question?
7. **Limitations** — at least one, stated specifically, not generically.
8. **Conclusion & next steps** — what you'd try with more time/data/compute.
9. **Questions** — invite audience questions.

## Presentation Tips
- Practice the timing — 7–10 minutes goes faster than it sounds; cut detail, not clarity.
- Lead with the motivating question, not the implementation — the audience needs to care about
  the "why" before the "how."
- The limitations slide (7) is required, not optional — it is what the rubric
  (`assignments/capstone-rubric.md`) specifically rewards beyond a single reported number.
- Be honest about what didn't work — a well-analyzed negative or mixed result scores as well as
  a clearly positive one.
- If your project reports results across multiple random seeds, show the spread (not just a
  mean) on at least one results slide.
