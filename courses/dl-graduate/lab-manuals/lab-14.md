# Lab Manual 14 — Capstone Topic Finalization and Literature Search

**Duration:** 3 hours | **Prerequisite:** Week 14 lecture

## Objectives
Finalize an individual or paired capstone topic and assemble an initial 3–5 paper candidate
reading list, applying the Week 14 structured-critique checklist to each candidate.

## Setup
No code environment required beyond a text editor/notebook for notes. Have
`assignments/capstone-proposal-guidelines.md` open for reference.

## Procedure
1. **Task A — Topic selection:** from the Week 1–13 topic menu, select a specific, concrete
   capstone subtopic (not "study diffusion models" but, e.g., "compare DDPM noise-schedule
   choices on 2-D synthetic data").
2. **Task B — Candidate literature search:** identify 3–5 candidate papers related to the chosen
   subtopic; for each, record title/authors/venue/year as best known.
3. **Task C — Rapid critique pass:** for each candidate paper, apply the Week 14 efficient-reading
   order (abstract → figures/results → method) and write 2–3 sentences on its central claim.
4. **Task D — Draft motivating question:** write one concrete, falsifiable motivating question
   the capstone experiment will investigate, and a one-paragraph sketch of the planned
   experiment.

## Expected Output
A short document (notebook markdown cells or a `.md` file) with Tasks A–D, ready to be adapted
directly into the capstone proposal.

## Submission
Submit the Task A–D document by the end of the lab session; this feeds directly into the
capstone proposal due this week (see `course-plan.md` §6 and `assignments/capstone-proposal-guidelines.md`).
