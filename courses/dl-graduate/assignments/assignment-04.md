# Assignment 4 — Paper Critique and Presentation (Weeks 14–15)

**Weight:** 10% of course grade | **Assigned:** Week 14 | **Critique due:** Week 15 | **In-class
presentation:** Week 15

## Overview
Individually (pairs are **not** permitted for this assignment — the Research Capstone, which may
be done in pairs, is the appropriate venue for collaborative work), choose one real, published
deep learning paper related to a topic from this course and produce a written critique plus a
short in-class presentation. This is the dedicated, graded application of Week 14's
research-methods skills, and is distinct from, and in addition to, the Research Capstone.

## Choosing a Paper
Select a real, published deep learning paper from a suggested-topics list — for example
(suggested starting points, not a required list; any real paper on a relevant subtopic, selected
in consultation with the instructor, is acceptable):
- A paper on an advanced CNN or Transformer architecture variant not covered in depth in lecture.
- A paper on self-supervised/contrastive representation learning.
- A paper on normalizing flows, diffusion models, or another modern generative modeling approach.
- A paper on graph neural networks (message passing, GCN/GraphSAGE/GAT variants, or an
  application area such as molecule property prediction).
- A paper on deep reinforcement learning (value-based, policy-gradient, or actor-critic methods).
- A paper on large-scale training practices or parameter-efficient fine-tuning.
- A paper on multimodal/vision-language or foundation models.
Locate your chosen paper through a current NeurIPS, ICML, or CVPR-style venue, or a reputable
preprint server; confirm your choice with the instructor by the start of Week 15.

## Written Critique (submit by Week 15)
A 1.5–2 page written critique (`critique.pdf` or `.md`) following the Week 14 checklist:
1. The paper's precise central claim, stated as a single falsifiable sentence.
2. The evidence offered (experiments, ablations, proofs) and whether it actually supports the
   claim.
3. Whether the baselines compared against are fair and current for the paper's publication
   venue/year.
4. Whether the paper isolates which part of its method drives the reported improvement
   (ablations).
5. At least one concrete deep-learning-specific reproducibility concern (compute cost,
   hyperparameter sensitivity, or benchmark-culture issue) from Week 14.
6. Full bibliographic citation of the paper (authors, title, venue/year as published).

## In-Class Presentation (Week 15)
A 5-minute presentation (strict time limit, 2 minutes Q&A) covering the claim, the evidence, and
your own assessment of its limitations — aimed at your classmates, who may not have read the
paper.

## Grading Rubric
| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Claim correctly and precisely identified | 20 |
| Evidence and baseline/ablation assessment, specific and well-reasoned | 25 |
| Reproducibility concern(s) identified, specific (not generic) | 15 |
| Overall assessment is a defensible, non-overstated judgment | 15 |
| Presentation (clarity, timing, honest Q&A) | 15 |
| Proper citation of the chosen paper | 10 |
| **Total** | **100** |

## Submission
Upload `critique.pdf`/`.md` via the course submission system by the start of Week 15; present in
class during the Week 15 session. Late policy per syllabus (`course-plan.md` §7).
