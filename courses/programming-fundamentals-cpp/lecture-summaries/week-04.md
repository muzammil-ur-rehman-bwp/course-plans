# Week 4 Summary — Loops: `for`, `while`, `do-while`

**Key takeaways:**
- `for` suits counter-controlled loops (known iteration count); `while` suits sentinel-controlled
  loops (unknown count, condition checked before the body); `do-while` guarantees at least one
  execution (condition checked after the body).
- `break` exits a loop early; `continue` skips to the next iteration.
- Nested loops process grid-shaped data — a direct preview of 2D arrays.
- Off-by-one bounds and forgetting to update the loop variable (infinite loops) are the two most
  common loop bugs — always double-check both.

**You should now be able to:** choose the right loop construct for a task; write loops that
accumulate, count, and search; avoid off-by-one and infinite-loop errors.
