# Lab Notes 5 — Set-of-Support Resolution

**Concept recap:** SOS requires every resolution step to use at least one parent drawn from the
support set S (typically the negated goal) or a descendant of one; this remains
refutation-complete when T (the rest) alone is satisfiable, and prunes T-only resolvents.

**Common pitfalls:**
- **Forgetting to grow the support set**: once a new resolvent is derived from a support-set
  parent, it must itself join the support set for future steps — a checker that only ever
  resolves against the *original* S, never its descendants, will miss valid SOS derivations and
  wrongly report no refutation found.
- **Allowing a T-T resolution step to slip through**: the defining restriction is that *at least
  one* parent must be in (the growing) S — a loop that iterates over all clause pairs without
  explicitly checking this condition silently degrades to unrestricted resolution.
- Confusing "fewer resolvents generated" with "always faster" — SOS's benefit is pruning useless
  T-only resolvents, but on a clause set where T alone is already small, the saving may be
  modest; Task C's larger instance is designed to make the saving visible.
- In Task D, applying the ∀-rule only once when the derivation actually needs it applied with a
  different instantiation elsewhere — unlike Week 4's single-pass DL ∀-rule, FOL's ∀-rule may need
  repeated application with different terms.

**Debugging tip:** verify your plain `resolve` function alone first (Task B) on 2–3 known
clause pairs with hand-computed resolvents before layering the SOS restriction logic on top in
Task A.

**Instructor tip:** have students manually label, on paper, every resolvent generated by
unrestricted resolution as "would SOS have allowed this?" before running Task B's comparison —
seeing by hand which resolvents SOS prunes makes the completeness argument concrete.
