# Research Capstone — Proposal Guidelines

**Topic selection opens:** Week 7–8 (informal discussion with instructor) | **Formal proposal
due:** End of Week 8 | **Weight:** part of the Research Capstone (25% of course grade total)

## What to Submit
A 1–2 page proposal (`capstone_proposal.pdf` or `.md`) covering:
1. **Subtopic:** which advanced-KR subtopic from this course (or closely adjacent, with
   instructor approval) the project addresses — e.g., modal/temporal logic, description-logic
   tableau methods, automated theorem proving, ASP, belief revision, argumentation, Markov logic
   networks, knowledge graphs/embeddings, multi-agent epistemic logic, or ontology engineering.
2. **Motivating question:** what specific question or gap is the project investigating? (This
   need not be fully refined yet — Week 15 will sharpen it — but it must be more specific than
   "I want to study X.")
3. **Planned literature:** 3–5 candidate papers (title/authors/venue/year) the student plans to
   review; these may change as the literature review develops, but the proposal must show a
   genuine starting point, not a placeholder.
4. **Planned experiment:** a reproduced or extended experiment from one of the candidate papers,
   or a focused comparison/extension using techniques from this course — stated concretely enough
   that a feasible scope is clear (e.g., "reproduce the grounded-vs-preferred-extension divergence
   analysis from [paper] on 3 additional AF topologies I construct" is concrete; "study
   argumentation" is not).
5. **Team:** individual or pair, with each member's planned contribution if a pair.

## Approval
The instructor will respond within one week with approval or requested revisions. Projects must
build on techniques covered in this course; topics requiring deep neural-network, classical-ML,
or deep-learning content (owned by the sibling graduate courses) require instructor pre-approval
and must still center on this course's own techniques.

## Example Topics (for inspiration, not a closed list)
A new ALC-tableau optimization (e.g., a simple blocking strategy) compared against the baseline
Week 4 implementation on a set of synthetic concepts; an alternative ASP encoding of a
combinatorial problem compared for stable-model count/size against the Week 6 encoding; an
AGM-style revision operator (e.g., a different distance metric) compared against Dalal revision
on a battery of test cases; a different argumentation semantics (e.g., stable extensions)
implemented and compared against grounded/preferred on several AF topologies; a larger-domain
MLN grounding experiment examining how relative world probabilities scale with domain size; a
TransE variant (e.g., a different corruption strategy or distance norm) compared on link
prediction accuracy; a muddy-children-style public-announcement puzzle variant (e.g., partial/
private announcements) worked out and simulated; a focused reproduction of a specific result
from a current KR-venue paper on any topic covered this semester.
