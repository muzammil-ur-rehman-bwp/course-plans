# Paper Critique & Presentation Assignment (Week 13–15)

**Weight:** 10% of course grade | **Assigned:** Week 13 | **Due:** Written critique by Week 15;
in-class presentation during Week 15 seminar sessions

## Purpose
This assignment is the dedicated, graded application of Week 15's research-methods skills (read
early, in overview, when this assignment is handed out in Week 13): read a real KR research
paper, critique it using the structured framework, and present your critique to the class. It is
distinct from, and in addition to, the Research Capstone — the capstone involves a literature
review and an experiment on a topic of the student's choosing; this assignment is a focused
critique of a single paper.

## Choosing a Paper
Select one paper related to any topic covered in this course (modal/temporal logic, description
logics, automated theorem proving, ASP, belief revision, argumentation, Markov logic networks,
knowledge graphs, multi-agent epistemic logic, ontology engineering, or explainability) from a
current KR conference, an IJCAI/AAAI KR track, the journal *Artificial Intelligence*, or the
instructor-provided suggested reading list distributed in Week 13. The paper must be a real,
publicly available paper you can cite properly (title, authors, venue, year) in your
submission — do not submit a critique of a paper you cannot actually produce a citation for.

## Deliverable 1: Written Critique (approx. 500–800 words)
Using the Week 15 structured-critique framework, address each of the following explicitly:
1. **Claimed result:** state the paper's central formal claim precisely.
2. **Does the argument establish it?** Is the claimed property proved, or only argued
   informally where a proof (or proof sketch) would be expected?
3. **Representativeness of examples:** are the worked examples representative, or do they avoid
   the formalism's hard cases?
4. **Comparison to this course's formalisms:** how does the paper's proposal compare to the
   closest formalism covered this semester, and what does it genuinely improve on (if anything)?
5. **Semantic-precision reproducibility:** is the proposed logic's syntax/semantics fully and
   unambiguously specified? Are complexity claims proved or only asserted?
6. **Your overall assessment:** in 2–3 sentences, state whether you find the paper's central
   claim convincing, and why.

## Deliverable 2: In-Class Presentation (5–7 minutes + Q&A, during Week 15)
Present your critique to the class in the same structure as the written critique. You will field
questions from classmates and the instructor; answering accurately and honestly (including "I'm
not sure, the paper doesn't say" where true) is part of the graded presentation skill.

## Grading Rubric
| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Claimed result accurately and precisely identified | 15 |
| Argument-adequacy and example-representativeness assessment, specific and well-reasoned | 20 |
| Comparison to this course's closest formalism, substantive and accurate | 15 |
| Semantic-precision reproducibility concern(s) identified, specific (not generic) | 15 |
| Presentation: clear, within time, handles Q&A accurately | 25 |
| Proper citation of the chosen paper | 10 |
| **Total** | **100** |

## Notes
- This assignment is graded on the quality of critique, not on whether the chosen paper turns
  out to be "good" or "bad" — a well-reasoned critique of a strong paper (identifying its real
  strengths precisely) scores as well as a well-reasoned critique of a flawed one.
