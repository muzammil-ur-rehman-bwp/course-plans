# Week 7 Summary — Belief Revision and Update

**Key takeaways:**
- The AGM postulates (K∗1)–(K∗6, plus K∗7–K∗8) characterize rational belief revision: success,
  minimal change relative to plain expansion, coincidence with expansion when consistent,
  non-triviality unless φ is itself unsatisfiable, and syntax-independence.
- Dalal revision constructs an AGM-satisfying operator semantically: K∗φ's models are φ's models
  at minimum Hamming distance from K's models.
- Revision ("what do we now believe about a static world") and update ("how has the world
  changed") are genuinely different operations; Katsuno–Mendelzon update computes minimal change
  per model of K rather than one global minimum, and can diverge from revision's answer.

**You should now be able to:** state and check the AGM postulates on a worked example; implement
and apply Dalal's Hamming-distance revision operator; distinguish a revision scenario from an
update scenario on the same starting belief set.
