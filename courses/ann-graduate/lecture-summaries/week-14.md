# Week 14 Summary — Information-Theoretic Perspectives

**Key takeaways:**
- The information bottleneck frames a layer's representation as trading off a fitting term
  ($I(T;Y)$) against a compression term ($I(X;T)$), conjecturing a fitting phase followed by a
  compression phase during training.
- Reported compression-phase evidence depends heavily on activation-function choice (seen with
  tanh, much less with ReLU) and on contested binned mutual-information estimators — this is an
  actively disputed research area, not settled theory.
- A disputed idea should still be presented with its precise claim, its evidence, and its named
  critiques — never asserted as fact or dismissed outright.

**You should now be able to:** state the information bottleneck's fitting/compression tradeoff
and explain, with specifics, why its strongest empirical claims remain disputed.
