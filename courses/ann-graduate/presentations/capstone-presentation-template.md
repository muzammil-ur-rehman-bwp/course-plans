# Capstone Presentation Template (Student-Facing)

**Format:** Conference-style talk, 10–12 minutes + 3–5 minutes Q&A (confirm exact timing against
`assignments/capstone-rubric.md` and the Week 16 schedule).

Use this as a starting outline — adapt slide count to your content, but keep every section below.

1. **Title slide** — project title, your name(s), course, date
2. **Motivation** — why this subtopic matters; one sentence on where it sits among this course's
   six theoretical pillars
3. **The gap/question** — the specific question your literature review identified as open or
   underexplored (not a restated topic — a question with a potential answer)
4. **Related work** — your 3–5 reviewed papers, each summarized in one line (claim + method), with
   correct attribution
5. **Your approach** — what you reproduced, extended, or derived, and why this specific choice
   addresses the gap from slide 3
6. **Experimental/derivation setup** — the independent variable, the baseline/comparison point,
   and (if stochastic) how many seeds and how spread is reported
7. **Results** — reported honestly, including any negative or partial results; show the actual
   data/plot, not just a verbal summary
8. **Analysis** — what the results do and do not show; explicitly distinguish what you have
   established from what remains open
9. **Limitations** — scope, scale, or methodological limitations of your own work
10. **Conclusion and future work** — one or two sentences each
11. **Questions** — invite audience Q&A

**Presenter notes:**
- Slide 3 (the gap/question) and slide 8 (analysis) are the two slides peer reviewers and the
  instructor will scrutinize most closely — a vague gap statement or an analysis that overclaims
  beyond the data are the two most common ways an otherwise solid project loses rubric points.
- Reporting a negative or partial result honestly on slide 7, analyzed soundly on slide 8, is
  explicitly not a penalty under the capstone rubric — an impressive-looking but poorly-supported
  claim scores worse.
- Practice against the time limit; a talk that runs over cuts into Q&A time and the next
  presenter's slot.
