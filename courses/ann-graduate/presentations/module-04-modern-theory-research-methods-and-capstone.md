# Presentation: Module 4 — Modern Theory, Research Methods & Capstone (Weeks 12–16)

**Format:** Slide deck outline for lecture delivery.

1. **Title slide** — Module 4: Modern Theory, Research Methods & Capstone
2. **The Neural Tangent Kernel, defined** — $\Theta(x,x';\theta)=\nabla_\theta f(x)^\top\nabla_\theta f(x')$
3. **Jacot, Gabriel, and Hongler's result** — infinite width $\Rightarrow$ kernel regression
   against a fixed kernel
4. **What NTK explains, and its limits** — easy trainability, but no feature learning
5. **The Lottery Ticket Hypothesis** — sparse winning tickets within a dense network
6. **Iterative magnitude pruning** — the procedure, step by step
7. **The initialization-dependence evidence** — winning ticket vs. random-reinit control
8. **Model compression & distillation** — pruning and teacher-student distillation, briefly
9. **The information bottleneck idea** — fitting vs. compression tradeoff
10. **The evidence and the dispute** — activation-function dependence; estimator-sensitivity
    critiques
11. **How to critique a paper** — claim, assumptions, evidence strength, limitations
12. **Reproducibility in ML research** — seeds, spread, documented hyperparameters, fair baselines
13. **The capstone, structurally** — literature review, experiment/derivation, paper, presentation
14. **Module and course recap** — three partial modern theories (NTK, Lottery Ticket, information
    bottleneck), each explaining a piece, none a complete theory; the six-pillar course map,
    revisited one final time

**Speaker notes:** slides 4, 7, and 10 each end on an explicit limitation or dispute — resist any
temptation to present NTK, Lottery Ticket, or information bottleneck as settled, complete
theories; the module's integrity depends on holding each one to the same evidentiary standard
taught in slide 11's critique checklist, which students then apply to their own capstone sources.
