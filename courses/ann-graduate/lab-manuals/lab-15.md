# Lab Manual 15 — Paper Critique and Capstone Reproducibility Assessment

**Duration:** 3 hours | **Prerequisite:** Week 15 lecture

## Objectives
Apply the paper-critique checklist to your own chosen capstone paper(s), and produce a written
reproducibility assessment of your capstone's planned experiment.

## Setup
Create `lab15.md` (or `.ipynb` if your plan includes code). Have your capstone's candidate papers
on hand.

## Procedure
1. **Task A — Critique, applied:** for each of your capstone's 3–5 candidate papers, write the
   four-part critique (claim, assumptions, evidence strength, limitations) from the Week 15
   lecture checklist, one short paragraph per paper.
2. **Task B — Gap statement:** from Task A's critiques, write one or two sentences stating the
   specific gap or question your capstone experiment/derivation will address, and explain why it
   follows from what the papers do *not* already establish.
3. **Task C — Reproducibility plan:** for your planned experiment, state: what you will vary
   (independent variable), what you will measure, how many random seeds you will run (if
   stochastic) and how you will report their spread, and what a fair baseline/comparison point is.
4. **Task D — Risk check:** identify one concrete way your planned experiment could fail to show
   anything interesting (a null result, a confound, an infeasible compute/time budget) and state
   what you would do if that happens.

## Expected Output
A short written document (Tasks A–D) suitable for inclusion in, or as a direct precursor to, your
capstone proposal/draft.

## Submission
Submit `lab15.md` by the end of the lab session; this feeds directly into the capstone draft due
this same week.
