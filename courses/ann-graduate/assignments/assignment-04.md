# Assignment 4 — Paper Critique and Presentation (Weeks 11–15)

**Weight:** 10% of course grade | **Assigned:** Week 11–12 | **Critique due:** Week 15 | **In-class
presentation:** Week 15

## Overview
Individually (pairs are **not** permitted for this assignment — the Research Capstone, which may
be done in pairs, is the appropriate venue for collaborative work), choose one theoretical machine
learning paper related to a topic from Weeks 11–14 (expressivity and depth, the Neural Tangent
Kernel, the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis and pruning, or information-theoretic perspectives) and
produce a written critique plus a short in-class presentation.

## Choosing a Paper
Select a real, published theoretical machine learning paper — for example (suggested starting
points, not a required list; any real paper on a relevant subtopic, selected in consultation with
the instructor, is acceptable):
- A paper on the Neural Tangent Kernel or infinite-width network theory.
- A paper on the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis, network pruning, or sparse training.
- A paper proposing or critiquing an information-bottleneck account of deep learning.
- A paper on generalization theory for overparameterized networks (double descent, implicit bias,
  margin-based bounds).
- A paper on why skip connections or attention mechanisms affect optimization/expressivity.
Locate your chosen paper through the venues mentioned in `course-plan.md` §9 (NeurIPS, ICML, or a
reputable preprint server); confirm your choice with the instructor by the start of Week 12.

## Written Critique (submit by Week 15)
A 1.5–2 page written critique (`critique.pdf` or `.md`) following the Week 15 checklist:
1. The paper's precise central claim, stated as a single falsifiable sentence.
2. Every assumption the claim depends on (e.g., infinite width, a specific architecture, a
   specific data distribution).
3. The strength and kind of evidence given (proof, proof under stated assumptions evaluated
   empirically outside them, a single experiment, many seeds, one architecture or several).
4. The paper's own stated limitations, plus at least one limitation or open question you identify
   yourself that the paper does not explicitly discuss.
5. Full bibliographic citation of the paper (authors, title, venue/year as published).

## In-Class Presentation (Week 15)
A 5-minute presentation (strict time limit, 2 minutes Q&A) covering the claim, the evidence, and
your own assessment of its limitations — aimed at your classmates, who may not have read the
paper.

## Grading Rubric
| Criterion | Points |

| Presentation (clarity, timing, honest Q&A) | 15 |
| **Total** | **100** |

## Submission
Upload `critique.pdf`/`.md` via the course submission system by the start of Week 15; present in
