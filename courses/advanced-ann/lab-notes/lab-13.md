# Lab Notes 13 — Paper Critique, Precision Test, and Survey Annotation

**Concept recap:** this lab is the direct, hands-on application of Week 13's three research-
methods standards (paper critique, problem-statement precision test, survey synthesis) to the
student's own emerging capstone topic.

**Common pitfalls:**
- In Task B, writing a topic rather than a question on the first attempt ("I want to study
  scaling laws") — this is expected and normal; the point of the precision test is to *catch* this
  and force a revision, not to get it right immediately.
- In Task C, giving a peer's draft a pass/fail verdict with no specifics — "yes, this passes" or
  "no, this doesn't work" without stating exactly what evidence would (or would not) resolve the
  statement as written is not useful feedback and does not satisfy the task.
- In Task D, summarizing each paper in isolation and skipping the relating-to-each-other
  requirement — five accurate, isolated one-paragraph summaries with no connecting sentence is
  explicitly *not* sufficient per the Week 13 §3 survey standard.

**Debugging tip:** if your Task B problem statement still feels vague after one revision, try the
"what experiment would I run" test — if you cannot describe even a rough experiment or analysis
that would bear on the question, it is probably still a topic, not a question.

**Instructor tip:** circulate during Task D annotation work and spot-check that at least one
relating sentence per paper names a *specific* other paper on the student's own list, not a vague
reference to "other work in this area."
