# Lab Notes 14 — Position Paper: A Current Open Problem

**Concept recap:** the Week 14 discipline is stating precisely what is established (in restricted
cases) versus what remains open (the gap to a general, useful answer) — neither "nothing is
known" nor "this is basically solved."

**Common pitfalls:**
- Restating the open question (Task A) at the same level of vagueness as the original handout
  rather than genuinely restating it in the student's own words — a near-verbatim copy does not
  satisfy Task A.
- Collapsing Task B into a single claim rather than two distinct parts — a common failure is
  writing only about what is open and skipping a clear statement of what the established
  restricted-case results actually are.
- In Task C, proposing a sub-question so broad it fails the same precision test introduced in
  Week 13 — apply that test here too, even though it is not explicitly required by Task C's
  instructions, since Task D's capstone-connection assessment depends on it.

**Debugging tip:** if Task C feels stuck, try explicitly listing 2–3 candidate partial answers
before choosing which to argue for — writing down the alternatives first often clarifies which one
is genuinely most promising versus merely the first one that came to mind.

**Instructor tip:** use Task D responses to identify students whose capstone topic may be
converging early, and offer brief individual consultation before the Week 15 work session for
students whose Task D response suggests they are still unsettled.
