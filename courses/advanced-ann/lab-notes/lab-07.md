# Lab Notes 7 — Position Paper: Competing Hypotheses for Grokking

**Concept recap:** both competing hypotheses are consistent with the three-phase curve; the task
is distinguishing evidence that would favor one over the other, not re-arguing the curve itself.

**Common pitfalls:**
- Writing Task C as a restatement of Week 7's lecture content rather than proposing a genuinely
  *new* piece of evidence — the exercise specifically requires identifying evidence not already
  covered in lecture; re-describing the weight-decay or circuit-formation evidence already given
  in class does not satisfy Task C.
- Treating one hypothesis as obviously correct and writing a one-sided paper — the Week 7 lecture
  content is explicit that neither is currently the single settled explanation; a strong Task C
  response engages the genuine uncertainty rather than resolving it by assertion.
- In Task D, giving vague feedback ("good point, I'll think about it more") instead of a
  substantive rebuttal-response that actually engages the specific counterpoint received.

**Debugging tip:** if you're stuck on Task C, try asking what observation would be *surprising*
under hypothesis 1 but *expected* under hypothesis 2 (or vice versa) — asymmetric predictions are
usually the easiest kind of distinguishing evidence to identify.

**Instructor tip:** during peer exchange (Task D), actively pair students who took different
"most promising hypothesis" stances on Task C where possible — this produces sharper rebuttals
than pairing students who largely agree.
