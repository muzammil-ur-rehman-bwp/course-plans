# Lab Notes 15 — Structured Peer-Review Workshop and Revision Plan

**Concept recap:** this workshop applies every research-methods standard from Week 13 (precision
test, survey synthesis, feasibility-argument rigor) to a peer's complete draft, then requires a
written, specific revision plan in response to feedback received on one's own draft.

**Common pitfalls:**
- Giving generic feedback ("this is interesting, maybe add more detail") instead of feedback keyed
  specifically to one of the four rubric components, per Week 15 §3's standard — generic feedback
  does not satisfy Task A/B.
- In Task C, listing feedback received without stating a concrete response to each item — a
  revision plan that only restates the feedback, without saying what the student will actually do
  about it, does not meet the task's requirement.
- Treating all feedback as equally authoritative — Week 15 §4 is explicit that the correct response
  evaluates each piece of feedback on its merits; a revision plan that commits to every single
  suggestion without any independent judgment is a weaker response than one that reasons about
  which feedback to act on and which to (with stated justification) decline.

**Debugging tip:** if you're struggling to give specific feedback on a peer's draft, work through
the four rubric components in `assignments/capstone-rubric.md` one at a time rather than reading
the whole draft and reacting holistically — the structured form exists precisely to prevent
feedback from collapsing into generic impressions.

**Instructor tip:** circulate during Task A and spot-check that feedback given on problem
statements actually applies the precision test explicitly (names what evidence would or would not
resolve the statement) rather than giving an impressionistic "this seems clear/unclear" verdict.
