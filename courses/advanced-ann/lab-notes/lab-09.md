# Lab Notes 9 — Position Paper: Statistical-Physics Analogies

**Concept recap:** the replica method and the spin-glass analogy are illuminating, partially
verified analogies with specific, statable limits — not a literal physical theory of real,
finite, trained networks.

**Common pitfalls:**
- Writing Task C as either "the physics proves this about real networks" or "the physics tells us
  nothing about real networks" — both overshoot the defensible, nuanced conclusion Week 9 §4
  argues for; a strong response lands somewhere specific and justified in between.
- Citing Week 9 §4's limits vaguely ("the assumptions aren't realistic") rather than specifically
  (naming which of the three stated limits — non-rigorous replica continuation, idealized-disorder
  mismatch, or simplified-architecture mismatch — applies to the chosen claim and why).
- In Task D, picking a counterpoint that merely restates the other student's position rather than
  identifying a genuine point of possible disagreement.

**Debugging tip:** if you're stuck on Task C, try drafting the strongest version of the *opposite*
conclusion from the one you plan to argue, then explain specifically why you find it less
convincing — this often surfaces the sharpest version of your own argument.

**Instructor tip:** encourage students who chose different claims (replica method vs. spin-glass
analogy) to pair up for Task D — the cross-claim comparison tends to produce a more interesting
exchange than two students defending the same claim.
