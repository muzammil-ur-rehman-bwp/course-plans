# Lab Manual 13 — Paper Critique, Problem-Statement Precision Test, and Survey Annotation

**Duration:** 3 hours (research-methods workshop) | **Prerequisite:** Week 13 lecture

## Objectives
Critique a provided theoretical ML paper excerpt; draft and precision-test a one-paragraph
problem statement for your own capstone candidate topic; annotate 5+ candidate related-work
papers using the synthesize-don't-just-list standard.

## Setup
No code environment is required this week unless your capstone topic needs it. Bring the
instructor-provided short theoretical ML paper excerpt from Week 13 lecture. Create a single
document `lab13_research_methods.md` (or `.pdf`).

## Procedure
1. **Task A — Paper critique:** using Week 13 §1's four questions (claim, assumptions, evidence
   strength, what is not established), critique the provided paper excerpt in 300–400 words.
2. **Task B — Problem-statement draft and precision test:** draft a one-paragraph problem
   statement for your own capstone candidate topic. Apply the Week 13 §2 precision test to your
   own draft in writing (could a knowledgeable reader state what evidence would resolve it, from
   the statement alone?). If it fails, revise it until it passes, and show both versions.
3. **Task C — Peer precision-test exchange:** exchange your Task B draft with a peer. Each of you
   applies the precision test to the other's draft and gives written feedback on whether it
   passes, and if not, what specifically is missing.
4. **Task D — Survey annotation (5+ papers):** identify 5 or more candidate related-work papers
   for your capstone topic, and for each, write 2–3 sentences stating its actual claim and finding
   (not a title/abstract paraphrase) plus one sentence relating it to at least one other paper on
   your list (who builds on whom, where they agree or conflict).

## Expected Output
A single document containing Tasks A–D as clearly labeled sections, including both versions of
the Task B problem statement and the peer feedback received in Task C.

## Submission
Submit `lab13_research_methods.md` (or `.pdf`) via the course submission system by the end of the
workshop session.
