# Lab Manual 9 — Position Paper: Statistical-Physics Analogies, Scope and Limits

**Duration:** 3 hours (critical-writing/discussion format) | **Prerequisite:** Week 9 lecture
(delivered after the Midterm Exam)

## Objectives
Precisely state one statistical-physics-derived claim about neural network loss landscapes, and
argue how confidently it should be read as applying to a real, finite, trained network versus the
idealized model it was actually derived for.

## Setup
No code environment is required this week. Bring the Week 9 handout (the replica-method idea and
the spin-glass critical-point analogy, summarized conceptually). Create a single document
`lab09_position_paper.md` (or `.pdf`).

## Procedure
1. **Task A — Claim restatement:** choose one of the two statistical-physics claims from Week 9
   (the replica method's validity for computing disorder averages, or the spin-glass critical-
   point-index/energy correlation) and restate it precisely, in your own words.
2. **Task B — Idealized-model identification:** identify, specifically, what idealized assumptions
   the claim's derivation depends on (e.g., a specific random-disorder distribution, a specific
   simplified architecture or infinite-width-style limit).
3. **Task C — Position paper draft (500–700 words):** argue how confidently your chosen claim
   should be read as applying to a real, finite, trained neural network on real data, citing Week
   9 §4's three specific limits precisely (not just by vague reference) and reaching a defensible,
   non-overstated conclusion.
4. **Task D — Peer exchange and rebuttal:** exchange drafts with a peer who chose the other claim
   (if possible). Each of you identifies one point in the other's position where a more confident
   (or more skeptical) conclusion could reasonably be argued. Append a 3–5 sentence rebuttal-
   response to your own paper addressing the counterpoint you received.

## Expected Output
A single document containing Tasks A–D as clearly labeled sections, ending with the Task D
rebuttal-response appended to the Task C draft.

## Submission
Submit `lab09_position_paper.md` (or `.pdf`) via the course submission system by the end of the
lab session.
