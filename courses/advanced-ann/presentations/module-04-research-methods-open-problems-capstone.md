# Presentation: Module 4 — Research Methods, Open Problems & Capstone (Weeks 13–16)

**Format:** Slide deck outline for lecture delivery (convert to slides in institution's template).

1. **Title slide** — Advanced Artificial Neural Network (Post Graduate): Module 4, Research
   Methods, Open Problems & Capstone
2. **Module recap** — Modules 1–3 covered this course's theoretical content; Module 4 is where
   that content becomes the foundation for original research
3. **Reading a theory paper critically** — claim, assumptions (often an idealized limit),
   evidence strength, what is not established
4. **The precision test** — could a reader state what evidence would resolve the question, from
   the problem statement alone?
5. **Survey-synthesis standard** — accurate representation; relating papers to each other;
   motivating a specific gap
6. **Feasibility arguments** — what must be true; the main risk, named honestly; why it's manageable
7. **How postgraduate evaluation differs** — a plan for research not yet completed vs. a completed-
   project rubric
8. **Current open problems, grounded** — feature learning's unified theory; simultaneously tight/
   invariant/computable bounds; grokking vs. double descent's shared-or-distinct mechanism
9. **The fast-moving-area caveat** — why the specific open-problems list is explicitly flagged as
   likely to date
10. **The research proposal's four components** — problem statement, 5+ paper survey, proposed
    approach, feasibility argument/preliminary results
11. **Peer-review workshop norms** — specific, actionable feedback keyed to each component
12. **Receiving feedback well** — evaluate on the merits; produce a written revision plan
13. **The capstone defense format** — qualifying-exam/thesis-proposal-defense style, not a
    conference talk
14. **Course map, full recap** — NTK revisited → mean-field → implicit bias → feature learning →
    sharpness/SAM → grokking → scaling laws → statistical physics → double descent → PAC-Bayes →
    mode connectivity/LTH revisited → research methods → open problems → capstone
15. **Where this leads next** — sibling postgraduate courses; NeurIPS/ICML/ICLR as ongoing venues
16. **Closing slide** — "A proposal that honestly names what remains open is stronger than one
    that overstates what is known" — the standard the capstone defense is held to

**Speaker notes:** this module's content is deliberately process-oriented rather than new
technical content — resist the temptation to add new derivations here; the module's job is to
convert 12 weeks of theoretical content into the research-formulation skill the capstone defense
actually tests.
