# Week 13 Lecture Plan — Advanced Artificial Neural Network (Post Graduate)
## Topic: Research Methods for Theoretical ML at the Postgraduate Level

**Duration:** 2 hours lecture + 3 hour seminar (research-methods workshop)

### Learning Objectives (Bloom's Level)
1. Critique a cutting-edge theory paper's claim, assumptions, evidence, and limitations.
   (*Analyze, Evaluate*)
2. Distinguish a genuinely open research problem from an incremental question already addressed by
   existing work, using a precision test. (*Evaluate*)
3. Draft a precise, falsifiable problem statement and a synthesized (not merely listed)
   related-work survey. (*Apply, Create*)

### Lecture Structure
| Time | Segment | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | Reading theory papers critically | Claim, assumptions (often an idealized limit), evidence strength, stated/unstated limitations |
| 0:30–1:00 | The precision test | Could a reader state what evidence would resolve the question, from the statement alone? |
| 1:00–1:30 | Survey-synthesis standard | Accurate representation; relating papers to each other; motivating a specific gap |
| 1:30–2:00 | Feasibility arguments | What must be true; the main risk, named honestly; why it's manageable |

### Materials/Equipment
- Slides: "Research Methods: Reading, Critiquing, Proposing"
- Handout: a short theoretical ML paper excerpt for guided in-class critique

### Formative Check (in-class)
Given a draft problem statement, apply the precision test and state whether it passes; if not,
rewrite it so it does.

### Link to Lab/Assessment
Lab 13: critique the provided paper excerpt; draft a one-paragraph problem statement for your
capstone candidate topic and subject it to peer critique; annotate 5+ candidate related-work
papers (see `lab-manuals/lab-13.md`). **Paper Critique & Presentation assigned (due Week 15).**
