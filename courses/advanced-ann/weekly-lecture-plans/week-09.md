# Week 9 Lecture Plan — Advanced Artificial Neural Network (Post Graduate)
## Topic: Midterm Exam; Statistical-Physics Approaches to Neural Network Theory

**Duration:** 2 hours exam + 3 hour seminar (critical-writing/discussion format)

### Learning Objectives (Bloom's Level)
1. Demonstrate qualifying-exam-level mastery of Weeks 1–8 (Midterm Exam). (*Remember, Understand,
   Analyze, Evaluate*)
2. Explain the replica-method idea and spin-glass analogies for loss-landscape structure at a
   conceptual level. (*Understand*)
3. Critically evaluate what these borrowed statistical-physics arguments do and do not establish
   about real, finite neural networks. (*Evaluate*)

### Lecture Structure
| Time | Segment | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–2:00 | Midterm Exam | Closed-book/open-notes per instructor policy; covers Weeks 1–8 |
| — | (seminar, separate session) | — |
| 0:00–0:30 | The replica-method idea | Disorder averages; the "several coupled copies" trick, conceptually |
| 0:30–1:00 | Spin-glass analogies | Random-matrix/complexity arguments for critical points, at their statistical-physics source |
| 1:00–1:30 | The honest limits | Real networks are not literally disordered physical systems; idealized assumptions the physics analysis requires |
| 1:30–2:00 | Synthesis | What a physics analogy illuminates vs. what it literally proves |

### Materials/Equipment
- Midterm exam paper
- Slides: "Statistical Physics and the Loss Landscape" (conceptual, no physics prerequisite assumed)

### Formative Check (in-class, seminar)
State, in one or two sentences, one assumption the spin-glass analogy requires that a real,
finite, data-trained neural network does not exactly satisfy.

### Link to Lab/Assessment
Lab 9: a critical-writing position paper on the replica-method/spin-glass analogies' scope and
limits (see `lab-manuals/lab-09.md`). No code required this week.
