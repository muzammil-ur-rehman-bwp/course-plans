# Week 1 Lecture Plan — Advanced Artificial Neural Network (Post Graduate)
## Topic: Course Overview — The Neural-Network-Theory Research Frontier

**Duration:** 2 hours lecture + 3 hour research seminar/lab

### Learning Objectives (Bloom's Level)
1. Recall the graduate-ANN-theory formalism this course assumes (universal approximation, autodiff
   formalism, initialization/normalization theory, optimization-landscape theory, classical
   PAC/VC/Rademacher generalization theory, and a first survey of NTK/Lottery-Ticket/information-
   bottleneck ideas). (*Remember*)
2. Map this course's research-frontier topics onto the broader neural-network-theory landscape,
   and explain how this course's scope is disjoint from the sibling postgraduate courses.
   (*Understand*)
3. State, in outline, a research proposal's four components (problem statement, related-work
   survey, proposed approach, feasibility argument). (*Understand*)

### Lecture Structure
| Time | Segment | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | Course intro | Syllabus walkthrough, grading, capstone-proposal preview |
| 0:15–0:45 | Assumed-foundations review | Rapid-fire recap checklist: universal approximation, autodiff, initialization/normalization theory, optimization-landscape theory, classical generalization theory, NTK/Lottery-Ticket/information-bottleneck survey — flagged explicitly as assumed, not re-taught |
| 0:45–1:20 | Research-frontier map | Slide walkthrough: this course's topics (NTK in depth, mean-field theory, implicit bias, feature learning, sharpness/SAM, grokking, scaling laws, statistical physics, double descent, PAC-Bayes, mode connectivity, LTH revisited) and where they sit relative to the graduate course |
| 1:20–1:50 | Proposal scoping, previewed | Introduce the four components of a research proposal at a high level, to frame the whole semester |
| 1:50–2:00 | Q&A | Open floor |

name one graduate-course topic it assumes as background without re-deriving it.
