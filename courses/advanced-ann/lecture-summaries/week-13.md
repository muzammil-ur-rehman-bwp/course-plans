# Week 13 Summary — Research Methods for Theoretical ML at the Postgraduate Level

**Key takeaways:**
- Critiquing a theory paper means precisely stating its claim, its assumptions (often an idealized
  limit), the strength of its evidence, and what it does not establish.
- The precision test for a problem statement: could a knowledgeable reader state what evidence
  would resolve it, from the statement alone, and is it not already settled by covered material?
- A related-work survey must synthesize (relate papers to each other, motivate a specific gap), not
  just list accurate summaries; a feasibility argument must name the main risk honestly, not just
  list reasons for optimism.

**You should now be able to:** critique a theory paper using the four-question framework; apply
the precision test to a problem statement; distinguish a synthesized survey from a list of
summaries; construct a feasibility argument that names a real risk.
