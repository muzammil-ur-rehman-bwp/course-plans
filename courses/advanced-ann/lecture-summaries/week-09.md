# Week 9 Summary — Midterm Exam; Statistical-Physics Approaches

**Key takeaways:**
- The Midterm Exam covered Weeks 1–8 at a qualifying-exam standard of explain-and-critique, not
  just recall.
- The replica method is a formal technique (average $Z^n$, then analytically continue to $n\to0$)
  for computing disorder averages, historically from spin-glass physics, now also applied to
  neural-network loss landscapes.
- The spin-glass critical-point-index/energy correlation gives a physics-grounded account of why
  saddle points dominate generically while trained networks disproportionately find good minima —
  but the analogy has specific, statable limits (non-rigorous replica limit, idealized-disorder
  assumptions, simplified architectures) that keep it an illuminating analogy, not a literal theory.

**You should now be able to:** state the replica-method idea and the spin-glass critical-point
analogy conceptually; name three specific limits on how far the analogy should be trusted for real
networks.
